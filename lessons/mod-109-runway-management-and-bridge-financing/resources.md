# Resources — mod-109 Runway Management & Bridge / Alternative Financing

This is the reference stack that sits behind every chapter and exercise in the module. Sources are grouped by tier of authority. Paul Graham's *Default Alive or Default Dead* essay is the load-bearing tier-1 reference for chapters 1 and 8; the NVCA model documents and Delaware corporate law are the load-bearing legal reference for the down-round / recap / cram-down chapters (3 and 4); the specialty venture-debt BDC public filings and lender-published primers are the load-bearing reference for the venture-debt chapter (5); the SEC releases and rules are the load-bearing reference for the growth-stage instruments chapter (7). Every quantitative "market-standard" claim in the module (facility-size percentages, covenant patterns, warrant coverage, RBF effective-yield ranges, down-round frequency) should be re-verified against the most recent published quarter of the current-market sources (Fenwick / WSGR / Carta / Kruze / Bessemer / SVB survey) before it goes into a live memo or a live term-sheet negotiation.

Because the alternative-financing landscape moves faster than the priced-round market (new RBF providers, consolidation among venture-debt banks, changing SEC posture on growth-stage instruments), always name the specific report vintage or dated source when quoting a specific number. The `<!-- needs-research: ... -->` markers embedded in some chapters flag places where a claim should be re-verified against the most recent publication of a specific source before being cited.

## Tier 1 — Foundational primary sources

The load-bearing primary sources. Read these first.

- **Paul Graham — *Default Alive or Default Dead?*** (October 2015) — [www.paulgraham.com/aord.html](https://www.paulgraham.com/aord.html). The originating essay for the default alive / default dead classification the module's runway discipline is built on. Read once cover-to-cover; internalise the "on current growth and current burn, do you reach profitability before running out of cash" framing. Chapters 1 and 8 are direct extensions of this reference.
- **National Venture Capital Association — Model Legal Documents** — [nvca.org/model-legal-documents/](https://nvca.org/model-legal-documents/). The seven signed model documents underpinning every US Series-A financing. Relevant here specifically:
  - **Model Amended and Restated Certificate of Incorporation** — the charter mechanics for pay-to-play, senior preference, blank-check preferred, and shadow preferred. Chapters 3 and 4.
  - **Model Voting Agreement** — drag-along, board composition, protective-provisions class-vote thresholds that gate any recap or cram-down. Chapters 3 and 4.
  - **Model Investors' Rights Agreement (IRA)** — pro-rata / preemptive rights that apply to bridge and extension rounds. Chapter 2.
  - **Model Stock Purchase Agreement (SPA)** — the definitive-document format an extension of an existing preferred series is built on. Chapter 2.
- **Delaware General Corporation Law (DGCL) and the Delaware Court of Chancery caselaw on fiduciary duties in preferred-stock transactions**. Chapters 3 and 4 reference the doctrinal frame under which pay-to-play, senior-preference, recap, and cram-down transactions are challenged. Load-bearing cases:
  - *In re Trados Inc. Shareholder Litigation*, 73 A.3d 17 (Del. Ch. 2013) — the canonical reference on directors' duties to common when preferred and common have divergent interests in a sale or restructuring. Cited in chapter 4.
  - *In re Nine Systems Corp. Shareholders Litigation*, 2014 WL 4383127 (Del. Ch.) — recap-transaction fairness review under entire-fairness doctrine.
  - Delaware GCL §§ 141, 151, 152, 242 — the statutory framework for board authority, preferred designation, share issuance, and charter amendment. Referenced in the recap / cram-down mechanics in chapter 4.

## Tier 2 — The practitioner canon

Books and long-form works every runway-management-and-alternative-financing CFO should have read.

- **Brad Feld and Jason Mendelson — *Venture Deals: Be Smarter Than Your Lawyer and Venture Capitalist*** — Wiley, currently in the 5th or later edition. [venturedeals.com](https://www.venturedeals.com/). The single most-cited practitioner reference on venture-deal mechanics. Chapters on bridge structures, anti-dilution, and the mechanics of down rounds are directly relevant to chapters 2, 3, and 4 of this module.
- **Scott Kupor — *Secrets of Sand Hill Road: Venture Capital and How to Get It*** — Portfolio, 2019. Inside-the-fund view of runway management, bridge dynamics, and the reserve-allocation decisions on the fund side of a bridge. Chapter 2's insider-set discussion benefits from Kupor's investor-side framing.
- **Bill Gurley — *Above the Crowd*** — [abovethecrowd.com](http://abovethecrowd.com/). Benchmark Capital partner. Key essays for this module:
  - *On the Road to Recap* — the canonical reference on preference-stack pathology at growth stages and the dynamics of recap events. Chapter 4.
  - *The New Venture Landscape and Where We Go From Here* and periodic market-condition essays — bear on the alternative-financing market context.
- **Mark Suster — *Both Sides of the Table*** — [bothsidesofthetable.com](https://bothsidesofthetable.com/). Long archive on bridges, dirty term sheets, and stressed-round mechanics. Chapters 2, 3, and 4 reference multiple Suster essays.
- **Fred Wilson — *AVC*** — [avc.com](https://avc.com/). Long-running blog by the USV managing partner. Multiple essays on runway management, "bridge from where to where," default alive framing (referenced by Graham), and USV's default-alive philosophy for portfolio-company advice.
- **Y Combinator essays and library entries on bridges and bridge SAFEs**. [ycombinator.com/library](https://www.ycombinator.com/library) — YC's published guidance on when SAFEs and notes fit for bridges, and YC's own advice to founders on runway management. Referenced by chapter 2.

## Tier 3 — Venture-debt specific sources

For chapter 5. The specialty lender BDCs are publicly listed and file quarterly 10-Q and annual 10-K disclosures on the SEC's EDGAR system, which are the most authoritative primary source on real venture-debt term structures in market.

- **Hercules Capital (NYSE: HTGC)** — [htgc.com](https://www.htgc.com/) and [SEC EDGAR filings](https://www.sec.gov/cgi-bin/browse-edgar?action=getcompany&CIK=0001280776). Publicly traded BDC. 10-K, 10-Q, and investor presentations disclose portfolio-company loan structures at an aggregate level and highlight case examples. The most granular publicly-available venture-debt term data set.
- **TriplePoint Venture Growth BDC Corp. (NYSE: TPVG)** — [tpvg.com](https://www.tpvg.com/) and [SEC EDGAR filings](https://www.sec.gov/cgi-bin/browse-edgar?action=getcompany&CIK=0001580345). Publicly traded BDC focused on growth-stage venture debt. Investor materials describe deal-structure patterns.
- **Runway Growth Finance Corp. (Nasdaq: RWAY)** — [runwaygrowth.com](https://www.runwaygrowth.com/) and [SEC EDGAR filings](https://www.sec.gov/cgi-bin/browse-edgar?action=getcompany&CIK=0001653384). Publicly traded BDC focused on later-stage venture debt.
- **Horizon Technology Finance (Nasdaq: HRZN)**, **Trinity Capital (Nasdaq: TRIN)**, and other listed venture-lending BDCs — [SEC EDGAR](https://www.sec.gov/edgar) filings available for each.
- **Silicon Valley Bank** — historical anchor of the venture-debt market; now part of **First Citizens BancShares** following the March 2023 failure and receivership. Post-acquisition operations are conducted as **Silicon Valley Bank, a division of First Citizens Bank & Trust Company** — [svb.com](https://www.svb.com/). SVB continues to publish market reports and startup surveys through the SVB State of the Markets series.
- **HSBC Innovation Banking** — [hsbc.com](https://www.hsbc.com/) → business banking. HSBC acquired SVB's UK arm and launched a broader Innovation Banking platform serving the venture-backed tech ecosystem globally.
- **Stifel Venture Banking** — [stifel.com](https://www.stifel.com/) → institutional group → venture banking. Post-SVB failure, several senior SVB bankers moved to Stifel, which built out a venture-focused practice.
- **Comerica Bank technology and life sciences banking** — [comerica.com](https://www.comerica.com/) → business banking. Longstanding venture-adjacent commercial bank; still competes in venture-debt for smaller facilities.
- **Kruze Consulting — venture-debt content** — [kruzeconsulting.com](https://kruzeconsulting.com/) → resources. Kruze publishes accessible primers on venture-debt structures, warrant coverage, and covenant patterns oriented at founders and startup CFOs.
- **Bessemer Venture Partners — venture-debt primers**. Bessemer's *State of the Cloud* content and adjacent Atlas / Roadmap publications include venture-debt commentary and market data. [bvp.com/atlas](https://www.bvp.com/atlas).
- **NVCA — Venture Monitor / Yearbook**, in cooperation with PitchBook — [nvca.org](https://nvca.org/). Aggregate venture-debt volume data at the industry level. Not detailed on deal terms but useful for macro context.
- **Frank Rotman (QED Investors) and specialty-lending investor commentary** — sector commentary on the BDC and specialty-finance landscape that provides the "how the lender thinks about it" side of the trade.

## Tier 4 — Revenue-based-financing sources

For chapter 6. RBF providers are largely private companies whose product terms are disclosed only in their own marketing content and in press coverage. Treat the specific provider list as time-varying and re-check current market participants when evaluating an RBF path.

- **Pipe** — [pipe.com](https://pipe.com/). Marketplace-based RBF for recurring-revenue businesses. Product mechanics and pricing described on the site; historical press coverage in TechCrunch, Bloomberg, and the FT for context on evolution of the product.
- **Capchase** — [capchase.com](https://www.capchase.com/). Direct-lender RBF for SaaS companies. Product and pricing on the site.
- **Founderpath** — [founderpath.com](https://founderpath.com/). RBF specifically for bootstrapped and small-VC-backed SaaS companies. Product and pricing on the site.
- **Arc** — [arc.tech](https://arc.tech/). Neobank plus RBF for early-stage software companies. Product and pricing on the site.
- **Uncapped** — [weareuncapped.com](https://weareuncapped.com/). RBF operating in Europe and the US.
- **Wayflyer** — [wayflyer.com](https://wayflyer.com/). E-commerce-focused RBF.
- **Clearco (formerly Clearbanc)** — [clear.co](https://clear.co/). E-commerce RBF; underwent significant restructuring in 2022-2023. Historical case study for the "RBF-as-substitute-for-equity" failure mode.
- **Lighter Capital** — [lightercapital.com](https://www.lightercapital.com/). Longer-established RBF and structured-debt provider for SaaS.
- **a16z Fintech / Bessemer / Point Nine content on RBF**. Investor-side commentary on the RBF category, its structural fit, and where it has and has not worked. Useful for the "right instrument vs. wrong instrument" framing in chapter 6.
- **TechCrunch and The Information coverage of the RBF category** — periodic longer-form pieces on category dynamics, provider consolidations, and case-study failures.

## Tier 5 — SEC releases and rules for growth-stage instruments

For chapter 7. The load-bearing primary references are the SEC's own release material and the rule texts.

- **SEC Regulation D** — [sec.gov/answers/regd](https://www.sec.gov/answers/regd.htm) and the underlying rule text at [17 CFR §230.500 et seq.](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-17/chapter-II/part-230). Rule 506(b) and 506(c) exemptions used for PIPEs and pre-IPO institutional placements.
- **SEC Rule 144A** — [17 CFR §230.144A](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-17/chapter-II/part-230/subpart-A/section-230.144A). The resale exemption for qualified institutional buyers.
- **SEC Regulation A** (post-JOBS Act reform) — [17 CFR §230.251 et seq.](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-17/chapter-II/part-230/subpart-A/section-230.251). Tier 1 and Tier 2 exempt public offerings. Also see the SEC's overview at [sec.gov/education/smallbusiness/exemptofferings/rega](https://www.sec.gov/education/smallbusiness/exemptofferings/rega).
- **SEC Form S-3** and shelf-registration rules — [sec.gov/files/forms-3.pdf](https://www.sec.gov/files/forms-3.pdf). Reference for ATM programme establishment.
- **SEC Regulation M** — [17 CFR §242.100 et seq.](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-17/chapter-II/part-242/subpart-A). Trading-practice restrictions applicable to ATM sales.
- **SEC Regulation S** — [17 CFR §230.901 et seq.](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-17/chapter-II/part-230/subpart-A/section-230.901). Offshore-offerings exemption often paired with Rule 144A for cross-border deals.
- **JOBS Act (Jumpstart Our Business Startups Act of 2012)** — [Public Law 112-106](https://www.congress.gov/bill/112th-congress/house-bill/3606). Statutory framework for the emerging-growth-company IPO regime, expanded Regulation A (Reg A+), and other capital-formation reforms.
- **Economic Growth, Regulatory Relief, and Consumer Protection Act (2018) and subsequent Regulation A amendments** — the March 2021 amendment raising the Reg A+ Tier 2 offering limit from $50M to $75M is documented in SEC Release No. 33-10884.
- **FINRA rules on placement-agent and underwriter compensation** — [finra.org](https://www.finra.org/). Referenced for banker-fee benchmarking in PIPE and 144A transactions.

## Reading order for this module

If you are new to the runway-management-and-alternative-financing canon, this is the order to read:

1. **Paul Graham, *Default Alive or Default Dead?*** — the load-bearing conceptual frame. Read once cover-to-cover before starting chapter 1.
2. **NVCA Model Certificate of Incorporation and Model Voting Agreement** — the legal architecture behind chapters 3 and 4 recap and cram-down mechanics.
3. **Feld & Mendelson, *Venture Deals*** — the practitioner canon. Chapters on bridges, down rounds, anti-dilution, and venture debt.
4. **Bill Gurley, *On the Road to Recap*** — the canonical growth-stage-preference-pathology essay.
5. **Cooley GO** on bridges, pay-to-play, and down-round mechanics — compact and useful founder-guide articles for chapters 2, 3, and 4.
6. **Kruze Consulting venture-debt primer** — accessible entry point for chapter 5's material.
7. **Hercules / TriplePoint / Runway Growth 10-K filings** — read one recent 10-K from each to internalise the specialty-lender view of venture debt.
8. **SVB State of the Markets releases** — historical market conditions and startup-financing patterns.
9. **RBF-provider websites (Pipe, Capchase, Founderpath, Arc)** — read the marketing pages for each and internalise the product-mechanics variation across the category.
10. **SEC rule text for Reg D, 144A, Reg A+, and Reg M** — the primary-source reading for chapter 7. Skim the rule texts once; return to specific sections when a specific transaction is being modelled.
11. **Fenwick, WSGR, and Carta most-recent quarterly deal-terms reports** — for the current-market anchor on down-round frequency and bridge-round terms.
12. **Delaware caselaw** (*Trados*, *Nine Systems*) — the fiduciary-duty backdrop for cram-downs. Read the case summaries in a treatise or in law-firm commentary before diving into the opinions themselves.

Only after that canon should you rely on secondary practitioner content (Substacks, LinkedIn essays, conference talks). Every quantitative claim in a capital-plan memo or a term-sheet counter-proposal should trace to a tier-1 through tier-7 source, with the vintage named. Assertions that cannot be sourced are flagged `<!-- needs-research: ... -->` in the chapters; the same discipline should apply in every counter-memo the CFO writes.
