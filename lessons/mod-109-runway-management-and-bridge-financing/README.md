# mod-109 — Runway Management & Bridge / Alternative Financing

**Capital Instruments Between Priced Rounds and the Discipline That Keeps a Startup Alive Long Enough to Use Them**

**Track:** [Startup Finance & Fundraising](../../CURRICULUM.md)
**Stage:** SERIES-A / SERIES-B
**Hours:** 22 (8 chapters + 7 exercises + planned lab + planned quiz)

## What this module installs

Every previous module in this track has been about producing a defensible priced round: the accounting, the unit economics, the model, the cap table, the convertibles that seed it, the valuation, the fundraise motion, the term-sheet negotiation. This module is about what happens **between** priced rounds, when the model says the company will run out of cash before the next milestone, and the CFO has to choose from an actual menu of capital instruments — an insider-led bridge, a venture-debt facility, a revenue-based-financing line, or, in the harder cases, a down round with pay-to-play and a common-employee re-up. It is also about the ongoing planning discipline that prevents most of those decisions from having to be made under duress in the first place.

The founder-numbers slice — cash-out date, monthly burn, default alive / default dead — belongs to the `startup-foundations` prerequisite. This module layers CFO-grade planning and capital-structure depth on top of that slice. Specifically:

- **The 18-24 month runway planning frame** — the model that anchors the CFO's job, the monthly re-forecast discipline against actuals, and the specific trigger points (12 months out: scenario-plan warning; 9 months out: narrowing to two or three concrete plans; 6 months out: plan-B activation) that separate a company that survives a soft market from one that runs out of options.
- **Bridge round structures** — extension of an existing preferred at the last-round price, insider-led bridge SAFE at a friendlier cap, bridge convertible note with a maturity trigger, all with the signalling / dilution / next-round-price implications that determine which structure fits which situation and which investor base.
- **Down-round management** — pay-to-play mechanics (existing preferred forced to invest pro-rata in the down round or lose seniority / convert to common), senior-preference stacking that layers a new preferred class ahead of the existing stack, and the recapitalisation option that collapses the stack entirely to make room for new money.
- **The human and organisational work of a down round** — cram-downs of holdout investors, common-employee re-up grants that restore option-holder motivation after the priced value drops, and the founder-and-CFO communication practice that keeps the team functional through the process.
- **Venture debt** — the SVB / TriplePoint / Runway Growth / Hercules pattern: term loan sized as a fraction of the last equity round, interest-only period followed by amortisation, warrant coverage, financial covenants (minimum cash, minimum ARR growth), Material Adverse Change (MAC) clauses, and the trade-off vs. equity that determines when venture debt extends runway and when it accelerates a failure.
- **Revenue-based financing (RBF)** — the Pipe / Capchase / Founderpath / Arc pattern: capital advanced against future subscription cash flows or ARR, priced as an effective yield, non-dilutive and non-recourse (in most structures) — and the specific working-capital fit that separates the "right instrument" from the "wrong-instrument-substitute-for-equity" failure mode.
- **Growth-stage capital instruments** — ATM (at-the-market) offerings, PIPEs (private investments in public equity), Reg A+ "mini-IPO," and Rule 144A private placements — the menu a growth-stage or pre-IPO CFO evaluates against a traditional Series-C or crossover round, with the specific liquidity, signalling, and regulatory trade-offs of each.
- **The "default alive" discipline layered on top** — the operating trigger that requires the company to be net-cash-flow positive within its runway on best-case KPIs (or explicitly and consciously not), and the founder-and-CFO communication practice that anchors the fundraise / runway / burn conversation quarterly rather than in a rushed board meeting three months from cash-out.

The module owns the **capital-instrument selection and runway-management discipline** on an ongoing-company basis. The full **M&A / IPO / secondary transaction execution** work is deferred to [`startup-exit-curriculum`](https://github.com/ai-startup-curriculum/startup-exit-curriculum). This module hands off to that track when a decision is made to sell, IPO, or shut down.
