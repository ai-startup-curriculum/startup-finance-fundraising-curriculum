# Exercise 03 — Fundraising Funnel Tracking Authoring

**Estimated time:** ~4 hours to author the tracker and run the six-week simulation; ongoing time if used against a live raise.
**Prerequisites:** Chapter 3 (funnel design, cadence, honest disqualification, momentum narrative), plus exercise 02 (target list) — the funnel tracker sits on top of a specific target list.

## Problem statement

Author a funnel tracker for a live seed or Series-A raise, then drive it forward through six weekly simulated cohorts of the funnel. Each week the exercise gives you a set of "events" (a first meeting held, a partner meeting scheduled, a fund goes silent, a term sheet arrives) drawn from the target list from exercise 02, and you update the tracker, produce the weekly Monday snapshot, make disqualification decisions, and re-author the momentum narrative for the CEO to use.

The point of the exercise is to install the specific weekly-cadence discipline chapter 3 describes: real-time tracker updates, honest disqualification of slow-no funds, and a defensible momentum narrative that a partner asking "who else is looking?" can be answered with. A raise without this discipline runs out of runway faster than one with it.

## Scenario

Use the target list from exercise 02 (or one similar in shape — 12-25 funds tiered into tier-1 / tier-2 / tier-3). The company is running a seed or Series-A raise (pick one) with the round size and stage from exercise 01. Assume the process starts on week 0 (Monday of week 1).

### The six-week event script

Each week, the following events happen (invent additional realistic events as needed to reach a full funnel — the below is a spine, not the whole simulation). The event script is deliberately noisy so the funnel decisions are non-trivial.

**Week 1.**
- Warm intros go out to all 5-8 tier-2 funds.
- Two tier-2 funds respond with a first-meeting request. First meetings are scheduled for week 2.
- One tier-2 fund passes at the intro stage ("not our current focus").
- Tier-1 outreach is queued behind tier-2 (per chapter 2 sequencing).

**Week 2.**
- Two first meetings happen. One goes well (partner is engaged, asks for follow-up materials); one goes flat (partner is skeptical of the wedge).
- The two remaining tier-2 warm intros close their loops: one first meeting scheduled for week 3, one no-response.
- Tier-1 outreach begins — warm intros go out to 4 tier-1 funds.

**Week 3.**
- The "goes-well" tier-2 fund asks for a follow-up meeting with the sponsoring partner + one other partner (informal partner meeting). Scheduled for week 4.
- The "flat" tier-2 fund goes silent (no follow-up).
- The third tier-2 first meeting happens. Fund asks specific product-diligence questions; expresses interest in a follow-up in ~2 weeks pending the diligence.
- One tier-1 fund responds to the warm intro with a first meeting. Scheduled for week 4.
- One tier-1 fund declines the intro. Two tier-1 funds do not respond.

**Week 4.**
- The tier-2 partner-meeting-track fund has its partner meeting. Positive signal — partner says they want to bring it to full partnership next Monday.
- The tier-1 first meeting happens. Strong signal — partner asks for a partner-meeting scheduled for week 5.
- The "goes-silent" tier-2 fund gets a follow-up note from the CEO; no response.
- Second wave of tier-1 outreach — remaining 3 tier-1 funds intros go out.
- A tier-3 fund reaches out inbound (heard about the round through the founder network); first meeting scheduled for week 5.

**Week 5.**
- The tier-2 full-partnership meeting happens. Positive outcome — fund goes into diligence. Diligence-request list arrives; CFO handles.
- The tier-1 partner meeting happens. Strong signal — partner will bring to full-partnership next week.
- The tier-2 with pending product-diligence follows up; positive; asks for a partner meeting in week 6.
- The tier-3 inbound first meeting happens. Fund is not a natural fit but asks for a follow-up.
- Two additional tier-1 intros land — first meetings scheduled for week 6.
- The "goes-silent" tier-2 fund now three weeks silent. Disqualification candidate.

**Week 6.**
- The tier-1 full-partnership meeting happens; deal is approved for a term sheet; term sheet to be delivered by end of week.
- The tier-2 diligence process is nearing conclusion; verbal indication the fund will issue a term sheet by early next week.
- The tier-2 partner meeting happens; positive signal, requesting a full-partnership in 2 weeks.
- Two tier-1 first meetings happen; one strong signal, one lukewarm.
- One tier-1 fund's associate schedules a first meeting for week 7 (later stage of intake).
- The tier-3 inbound fund follows up asking to co-invest as a follower cheque if a lead emerges.

At end of week 6: term sheets from two funds imminent; CEO needs to run the close (exercise 07 territory).

## Requirements

For the six-week simulation, produce:

### 1. The funnel tracker itself

Structured per chapter 3, with one row per fund and the following columns at minimum:

- Fund name, sponsoring partner, tier.
- Intro requested date, intro received date.
- First meeting date, partner meeting date, full-partnership date, diligence start date, term-sheet received date.
- Current stage (Target list / Intro sent / First meeting scheduled / First meeting held / Partner meeting scheduled / Partner meeting held / Full partnership / Diligence / Term sheet / Passed / Disqualified).
- Next action, next-action owner, next-action due date.
- Status (Active / Passed / Slow / Disqualified).
- Signal notes (per-fund free-text notes, updated per week).

The tracker is updated in real-time across the six-week script. Every event above lands on the specific fund's row and moves the fund's stage / status / signal-notes accordingly.

### 2. Six Monday snapshots (one per week)

Per chapter 3's Monday review, each week produce a one-page snapshot with:

- Count of funds at each stage (funnel shape).
- Week-over-week deltas (which funds moved forward, which back, which entered, which exited).
- Top-three moving processes (funds most likely to yield a term sheet in the next 2-3 weeks).
- Top-three stalled processes (funds needing re-engagement or disqualification decisions).
- Disqualification decisions for the week — which funds are re-classified from Active to Slow or Disqualified, with reasoning.
- The CEO's momentum narrative for the week — a specific 2-3 sentence version of what to say when a partner asks "who else is looking?"

### 3. Disqualification log

A running log of every disqualification decision made across the six weeks, with:

- Fund.
- Date of decision.
- Signal that triggered it (explicit pass / slow-no pattern / silent-no / partnership-alignment failure).
- CFO's reasoning in 1-2 sentences.
- Any "door left open" note (follower cheque possibility, next-round conversation).

### 4. End-of-simulation retrospective (1 page, Markdown)

A short memo covering:

- Where the funnel ended (stages counts, term sheets in-hand, active processes).
- The per-stage conversion rates observed vs. the chapter 3 practitioner ranges. Diagnose any material off-range conversions.
- The lessons from the six weeks — which specific fund-level decisions the CFO would make differently in retrospect.
- The specific "what changes for close week" note — how the momentum narrative and process compression need to shift as the funnel enters the close window (setup for exercise 07).

## Starter guidance

- **Build the tracker before week 1's events.** Populating the tracker on the fly is chaotic; get the structure right first.
- **Update in real time as events happen.** A tracker updated at end-of-week is a snapshot; a tracker updated in real time is a live picture.
- **Do not skip the Monday snapshot.** The snapshot is what forces the CFO to actually think about the funnel state and not just record it.
- **Be honest about disqualification.** The chapter 3 discipline is that a fund with two weeks of no signal is a candidate for re-classification. If you find yourself calling every "goes-silent" fund "still active — just slow," you are running the founder's optimistic pattern, not the CFO's realistic pattern.
- **Write the momentum narrative every week.** The CEO needs it every week for the partner meetings. Write it, and re-write it as the funnel shape changes.
- **Cite the actual funnel numbers, not marketing numbers, in the narrative.** "We're in second meetings with three funds" — if the tracker says two funds and one is slow, the honest narrative is "we're in second meetings with two funds; a third is in a follow-up cycle."
- **Do the conversion-rate diagnostic at the end.** If the target-list-to-first-meeting conversion was 40% (below the 50-80% range from chapter 3), diagnose it: was it the intro paths, the deck, the timing, or the target list itself?

## Acceptance criteria

- **A completed funnel tracker** covering all events across the six-week script, updated per event.
- **Six weekly Monday snapshots** with the required sections.
- **A disqualification log** with per-decision reasoning.
- **The end-of-simulation retrospective** with per-stage conversion vs. practitioner-range diagnosis and specific lessons.
- **Momentum-narrative history** — the six specific momentum narratives produced week-over-week, showing how the narrative evolved as the funnel matured.
- **Honest classifications** — funds that go silent for two weeks or more are classified as Slow or Disqualified, not Active.
- **Owner attribution** on every next-action (CEO / CFO / intro-source), so the tracker is unambiguous about who is responsible for the next move.
- **The close-week setup note** — a specific handoff to exercise 07 (close choreography) identifying the top-two most likely lead funds and the specific week-7 actions needed.

## Deliverables

- The funnel tracker (.xlsx, Google Sheets link, or a Notion / Airtable equivalent).
- Six Monday snapshots (Markdown or slides).
- The disqualification log (Markdown or a tab of the tracker).
- The end-of-simulation retrospective (Markdown, ~1 page).

## Extensions (optional)

- **Add a signalling-risk log.** As the funnel matures, some passed funds may be sources of signalling risk (see chapter 7). Log which funds passed, what they know about the process, and any signalling-management actions taken.
- **Add a per-fund partner-attendance log.** Track which partners attended which meetings — this is a partner-fit signal that shifts probability-to-close.
- **Add a data-room-request log alongside the funnel.** As diligence-heavy funds request data-room artifacts, log the request date, fulfillment date, and any specific items that trigger a follow-up conversation.
- **Rerun the simulation for a Series-B raise** with a modified event script (extended timeline, third-party diligence involvement, executive-team-participation meetings). Compare and contrast the funnel shape and cadence.
- **Extend to week 7-8 (close week).** Author the close-week choreography as a preview of exercise 07 — how the funnel state at end of week 6 informs the term-sheet-window mechanics.
