# Exercise 02 — Investor Target List Construction Drill

**Estimated time:** ~4-6 hours
**Prerequisites:** Chapter 2 (target-list filters and partnership economics), chapter 1 (round sizing) — the target list is built against a specific round size, so exercise 01 or an equivalent sizing needs to be in hand.

## Problem statement

For a specific target company and a specific target round, construct a 40-fund investor target list passing all four filters from chapter 2 (thesis fit, stage / cheque-size fit, recent-portfolio-adds pattern, partnership-economics fit) and layered with partner-level fit for the top-tier funds. Rank the list into three tiers, document the partner-level fit hypothesis for the top 10, and produce a sequencing plan for outreach.

The point of the exercise is to install the discipline of building the target list against the *filters* rather than against brand names, and to run the partnership-economics fund-math on a real universe of funds so the math becomes second nature. A raise that starts with a brand-names list wastes founder-hours; a raise that starts with a filter-derived list is what the CFO defends the strategy for.

## Scenario — build your own

Pick one target company (real or hypothetical) and one target round. The company should have enough specificity that filters can be applied. At minimum:

- Sector (specific — e.g., "vertical SaaS for legal-tech mid-market," not "B2B SaaS").
- Business model (subscription / usage-based / transactional / marketplace / hybrid).
- Stage (seed / Series-A / Series-B).
- Target round size (from exercise 01 or an equivalent sizing) — e.g., $8M seed.
- Target pre-money and post-money (order-of-magnitude — this drives the fund-math filter). Cite the mod-106 valuation frameworks used to arrive at the pre-money band.
- Geography (headquarters, primary market, whether the company is US-centric or global).
- Founder profile in one paragraph (prior experience, prior fundraising).

Document the target-company / target-round profile on a "context" tab of the target-list workbook or as a header in the deliverable.

## Requirements

Produce a target-list workbook (spreadsheet), a tier-1 partner-fit annex, and a sequencing plan.

### Target-list workbook

Structured with one row per fund and the following columns:

| Column | Content |
|---|---|
| Fund name | The fund. |
| Fund size (current fund) | Deployed fund size. Cite source. |
| Vintage / fund number | Which fund (e.g., "Fund IV," 2024 vintage) |
| Stage focus | Declared stage focus (pre-seed / seed / Series-A / Series-B / multi-stage). Cite. |
| Sector thesis | The fund's stated sector thesis in one sentence. Cite source (fund website / partner blog / thesis publication). |
| Typical lead cheque at target stage | Range or median. Cite source (Crunchbase / PitchBook / fund's disclosed cheque size). |
| Lead vs. follow orientation | Lead-first / co-lead / follow-first at the target stage. |
| Last 8-12 leads (sector) | The dominant sector of the fund's most recent 8-12 lead investments. |
| Last 8-12 leads (stage) | Stages the fund has been leading at recently. |
| Ownership target | The fund's target ownership at the target stage (15-25% is common at Series-A). |
| Cheque as % of target round | The fund's typical cheque divided by the target-round size. |
| Ownership at target cheque | Resulting ownership stake under the target pre-money. |
| Fund-math verdict | Natural fit / stretch / mismatch. |
| Thesis-fit verdict | Pass / borderline / fail. |
| Recent-adds verdict | Pass / borderline / fail. |
| Overall tier | Tier 1 / Tier 2 / Tier 3 / Off-list. |
| Sponsoring partner (if known) | Named partner. |
| Partner-fit hypothesis | One-sentence hypothesis for tier-1 and tier-2 funds. |
| Warm-intro path | The specific intro path — mutual connection, portfolio-CEO connection, prior conversation. |
| Notes | Free-text notes on the fund's current signal. |

**Minimum universe:** 40 funds must pass through the filters. The output list may be smaller (as some fail the filters) — chapter 2 suggests 12-25 funds is a reasonable post-filter output.

### Tier-1 partner-fit annex

For each of the top-10 funds (typically all tier-1 plus the strongest tier-2), produce a half-page annex with:

- The specific target partner named.
- The partner's sector coverage — the last 3-5 investments the partner has led, and how they overlap with the target company.
- The partner's public content in the last 6-12 months (blog post, podcast, conference talk) — what themes the partner is emphasising.
- The specific partner-fit hypothesis for this deal — why this partner is likely to be the sponsor.
- The warm-intro path — specifically, who could make the intro and why they carry credibility with the partner.
- Two "why they'd sponsor" pieces of evidence and one "why they might not" caveat.

Cite specific sources — fund website, LinkedIn, blog URL, portfolio page — for every partner-level claim. Use `<!-- needs-research: ... -->` for any claim you cannot cite to a specific source.

### Sequencing plan

A one-page memo covering:

- The order of outreach across tiers (chapter 2 recommends tier-2 first to calibrate, tier-1 second).
- The specific first-wave targets (5-7 funds), their timing, and the intro paths.
- The pre-work per target — the specific artifact updates needed before the first meeting (deck version, demo state, model version, data-room state).
- The go / no-go criteria for the tier-2 calibration — what tier-2 feedback triggers a tier-1 outreach vs. a deck re-iteration first.
- The disqualification and re-tiering plan — how a fund moves from tier 1 to tier 3 in response to signal.

## Starter guidance

- **Start with the universe, not the tiers.** Pull an initial universe of 100-200 active funds from a source (Crunchbase / PitchBook / Signal by NFX / Harmonic / the founder-CEO's existing list). Apply the filters to that universe; the tiers emerge from the filtering.
- **Do the fund-math on every fund.** It's a 30-second calculation per fund and it changes the tier assignment for many. Do not skip it because the fund is "obviously right for us."
- **Use the fund's most recent 12-18 months of adds, not the historical portfolio.** The current-vintage adds are the real signal; the historical portfolio can be misleading.
- **Cite everything.** Every fund-size, cheque-size, thesis, and portfolio-add claim gets a source URL or a `needs-research` tag. This is a diligence-quality artifact, not a rough sketch.
- **Do not put brand names on the list because they are brand names.** Sequoia, a16z, Benchmark, Founders Fund, Accel, Kleiner Perkins pass the four filters only if the specific target company fits the specific fund's current thesis, stage, cheque size, and portfolio-adds pattern. If they don't pass, they don't pass. Do not soft-pedal.
- **Separate leads from followers.** Some funds will not lead the round but will follow. Mark them so the sequencing plan knows which funds to pitch as potential leads vs. potential followers.
- **Layer partner-fit only for tiers 1 and 2.** Doing partner-level research on all 40 funds is a time-sink. Tier 3 funds get a fund-level assessment; if they move to tier 2 or 1 later, add the partner-level layer.
- **Note the source for every non-obvious claim.** The reader (the founder-CEO, later a board member) needs to be able to verify the fund-math and the sector-fit claims independently.
- **This is a living document.** Set up the workbook so it can be updated weekly as the raise runs. New fund adds, partner departures, or fund-close events reshape the tiering.

## Acceptance criteria

- **A worked-through initial universe of at least 100 funds** with the four filters applied.
- **A post-filter target list of 12-25 funds** with each row completed for every column.
- **A tier-1 / tier-2 / tier-3 split** matching the chapter 2 guidance (5-8 / 5-10 / 5-10 funds).
- **The partnership-economics fund-math computed** for every fund, showing cheque size / ownership at target pre-money / fund-math verdict.
- **A tier-1 / top-10 partner-fit annex** with partner name, coverage evidence, public-content survey, fit hypothesis, and warm-intro path per fund.
- **Every non-obvious claim cited** with a source URL or a `needs-research` tag. No unsourced fund-size or portfolio claims.
- **A sequencing plan** with first-wave targets, calibration criteria, and disqualification rules.
- **A leads-vs.-followers split** documented for the funnel design (exercise 03 uses this).
- **A "why brand-name-X is not on the list" section** in the sequencing plan, if the reader would expect them — the specific filter each dropped on. Names like Sequoia / a16z / Benchmark / Founders Fund should be either on the list with a defence or off the list with a defence.

## Deliverables

- The target-list workbook (.xlsx or Google Sheets link).
- The tier-1 top-10 partner-fit annex (Markdown or PDF).
- The sequencing plan memo (Markdown, ~1 page).

## Extensions (optional)

- **Add a second, sector-adjacent target list** for a hypothetical alternative (a pivot in wedge — the same team but a different sector). Compare and contrast the tier-1 lists and note how much of the funnel would change vs. carry over.
- **Add a competitor-set portfolio-conflict analysis.** For every tier-1 fund, list the fund's portfolio companies that would be conflicts with the target company (competitive or near-adjacent). Mark any funds that likely fail on conflict grounds.
- **Add a fund-timing signal check.** For every tier-1 fund, note when the fund closed the current fund vintage. Funds that closed 4+ years ago are typically deep into deployment (fund is mostly deployed, follow-on-focused); funds that closed 0-2 years ago are typically actively deploying new leads.
- **Add a partner-recent-activity note.** For every named partner, note whether the partner has led a deal in the last 3-6 months (actively deploying) or has not led a deal in 9-12 months (slower deployment cadence, potentially heads-down on portfolio).
- **Add a "reverse target list" of funds you would not accept as a lead** even if they offered a term sheet — funds with a known-difficult reputation or a portfolio-conflict pattern. Cite the reasoning.
