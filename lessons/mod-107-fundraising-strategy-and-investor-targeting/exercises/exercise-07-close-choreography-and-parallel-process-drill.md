# Exercise 07 — Close Choreography and Parallel Process Drill

**Estimated time:** ~4-5 hours (design the close week and run the three contingency simulations).
**Prerequisites:** Chapter 7 (close choreography and parallel processes), plus the funnel state from exercise 03 (which leads into the close week).

## Problem statement

Design the close-week choreography for a Series-A raise with three converging investor processes. Produce the day-by-day playbook covering partner meetings, diligence-question closeouts, term-sheet arrival management, and CEO / CFO role split. Then simulate three specific contingencies — a slow-no from a would-be lead, a fast-yes with pressure to sign without shopping, and a signalling-risk incident from a passed fund — and produce the specific response playbook for each.

The point of the exercise is to install the choreography chapter 7 describes: parallel processes converging in a compressed window, honest management of signalling risk, and specific decision rules for when a lead's term sheet gets accepted vs. shopped. A raise that gets to two or three converging term sheets in-window and closes with negotiating leverage is different from a raise that closes with the first term sheet that arrives.

## Scenario — the pre-close state

At end of exercise 03 (week 6), the funnel has produced three serious processes converging toward term sheets:

- **Fund Alpha (Series-A specialist, tier-1).** Full-partnership meeting cleared. Term sheet expected end of week 7 (5 business days out). $12M lead cheque at $40M pre-money indicated verbally.
- **Fund Beta (multi-stage fund, tier-1).** In deep diligence. Verbal indication of term sheet imminent — timing unspecified but likely within week 7-8.
- **Fund Gamma (Series-A specialist, tier-2).** Second partner meeting mid-week 7; strong signal. Term sheet possible by end of week 8 if the second meeting goes well.

Also in play:

- **Fund Delta (passed at partner meeting, tier-1).** Passed 2 weeks ago citing "unclear wedge." Delta's partner is well-connected to Alpha and Beta partners — signalling risk exists.
- **Two follower funds** with indicated interest at $500K-$1M cheques if a lead emerges.
- The company has 4-5 months of runway remaining. Close pressure is real but not urgent.

The company is currently at $2M ARR, growing 8% MoM, NRR 115%. Round size targeted at $12M (from exercise 01 sizing). Founder-CEO is 4 years into the company; there is no full-time CFO — a fractional CFO (the student) is running the raise choreography.

## Requirements

### 1. Base-case close-week choreography (~2 pages, Markdown)

A day-by-day playbook for weeks 7 and 8, structured as chapter 7's close-week guide. For each day:

- **Meetings and calls** — who is meeting whom, with what agenda, and how long.
- **CFO tasks** — model deep-dives, data-room updates, reference-call coordination, term-sheet analysis prep.
- **CEO tasks** — partner conversations, momentum-narrative updates, reference-call anchoring.
- **Signal-watch checkpoints** — what to look for from each fund and what would trigger an intervention.
- **Decision gates** — specific moments where a decision has to be made (e.g., "Wednesday of week 8: if Beta term sheet has not arrived, escalate with a specific direct-timing question to the partner").

The playbook covers both weeks and reaches the intended close: a signed lead term sheet with follower cheques committed, closing in weeks 9-11.

### 2. Term-sheet-arrival playbook (~1 page, Markdown)

Chapter 7 mechanics: when the first term sheet arrives, what happens in the next 24-72 hours. Cover:

- **The 24-hour analysis** — CFO analyses the term sheet against the NVCA reference (mod-108 preview). Same-day summary to CEO and board (or informal seed-investor circle).
- **The response window** — how long before a substantive response goes back (chapter 7 guidance: typically 24-48 hours to acknowledge, longer for substantive negotiation).
- **The "who else to notify" protocol** — which other in-flight funds get a specific "we have a term sheet" note, with what language, and with what specific implication for their timing.
- **The board / advisor communication** — when and how the board or advisor circle is looped in for the term-sheet decision.

### 3. Contingency 1 — slow-no from Fund Alpha (~1 page, Markdown)

**The scenario.** By end of week 7, Alpha's term sheet has not arrived. Partner is polite but non-committal. Signals of a slow-no: emails going to associate not partner, specific diligence-close ambiguity, no partnership date reconfirmed. Meanwhile Beta is closer to a term sheet.

**Produce.**

- The specific pattern-diagnosis (chapter 7's slow-no signals — which of them apply, which don't).
- The specific CEO-partner conversation to force the timing question ("we're targeting a decision by X — is that a timeline that works for you?").
- The specific pivot playbook — reallocating founder-CEO attention from Alpha to Beta and Gamma, without prematurely disqualifying Alpha.
- The specific communication to the passed-fund-follower ecosystem — what to say about Alpha's status if a follower asks.
- The specific criterion for disqualifying Alpha vs. leaving them in a "slow-active" state.

### 4. Contingency 2 — fast-yes with pressure to sign from Fund Beta (~1 page, Markdown)

**The scenario.** Beta issues a term sheet on Tuesday of week 8 with a 48-hour signing window ("we need a decision by Thursday close of business"). Terms: $12M at $38M pre-money — slightly under Alpha's verbal — with a 2x liquidation preference and slightly more aggressive protective provisions than the NVCA standard.

**Produce.**

- The specific term-sheet analysis (per mod-108 preview): identification of the non-standard terms, the specific implications, the negotiation posture.
- The specific 48-hour pressure evaluation — is the pressure legitimate (fund's investment committee cadence) or is it a negotiating tactic? Chapter 7's guidance on pressure evaluation applies.
- The specific "buy time" playbook — what to say to Beta to extend the window without appearing to shop the term sheet or lose the deal.
- The specific "should we shop" decision framework (chapter 7: when to accept a lead's term sheet vs. shop it). Apply the criteria to the specific scenario.
- The specific communication to Alpha (still in "slow" state) and Gamma (in partner-meeting-track) if the CFO decides to shop.

### 5. Contingency 3 — signalling risk from Fund Delta (~1 page, Markdown)

**The scenario.** On Wednesday of week 8, the CEO gets a message from a friendly founder that Delta's partner mentioned to a peer at a dinner that "they passed on [company] — the wedge is unclear." The peer is now speaking to Beta's partner who is one of Delta's LP-side co-investors.

**Produce.**

- The specific signalling-risk assessment: how serious is this? Chapter 7's signalling-risk section applies.
- The specific damage-control playbook — is a direct outreach to Delta warranted? What would that outreach say?
- The specific counter-narrative for Beta and Gamma — assume the "unclear wedge" reference lands; how does the CEO reframe the wedge in the next partner conversation?
- The specific consideration for whether to close faster (accept Beta's terms) to reduce the window for further signalling damage, or to hold pace and shop.
- The specific lesson for future raises about pre-emptive signalling-management (chapter 7's guidance on managing passed funds through the process).

### 6. Retrospective (~1 page, Markdown)

After running the base case plus three contingencies, produce a memo covering:

- Which contingency was most likely to arise in a real raise of this shape, and why.
- The single decision most vulnerable to being made wrong under pressure (typically the "shop vs. accept" call).
- The specific pre-close preparation that would have made the contingencies easier to manage (e.g., pre-authoring the "we have a term sheet — here is our timeline" language for the notification-to-other-funds moment).
- The handoff to mod-108 (term-sheet mechanics) — which specific term-sheet analysis skills the contingencies exposed as necessary.

## Starter guidance

- **Design the base case first, contingencies second.** The base case is the intended path; the contingencies are deviations from it. Get the base clean before you introduce failure modes.
- **Use the tracker from exercise 03 as the state input.** The choreography is choreography of the specific funds at the specific stages the tracker records. Do not invent parallel funnels.
- **Do not accept the first term sheet without a specific decision.** Chapter 7's guidance is explicit: even a "good" first term sheet is a decision, not a default. Structure the decision as a decision.
- **Distinguish real pressure from negotiating pressure.** A 48-hour window from a fund with a monthly IC cadence is a legitimate pressure; a 48-hour window from a fund whose IC met yesterday is negotiating pressure. The CFO's job is to read which.
- **Note that shopping a term sheet is a norm, but a specific-context norm.** Chapter 7 covers the specific conditions under which shopping is expected and the specific conditions under which it is a norm violation. Apply the specific criteria.
- **Signalling risk is not always damage.** A passed fund saying "we passed" is normal. A passed fund saying "we passed because X" is more damaging — the specific "why" matters. Handle each accordingly.
- **The CFO owns the term-sheet analysis; the CEO owns the negotiation.** Chapter 8's split applies. Do not confuse the two.
- **Keep the retrospective specific.** "We would prepare better" is not a lesson; "we would have pre-drafted the term-sheet-notification-to-other-funds email and had it board-approved before the term sheet arrived" is.

## Acceptance criteria

- **Base-case day-by-day choreography** for weeks 7 and 8, with meetings, tasks, signal-watch, and decision gates.
- **Term-sheet-arrival playbook** covering the 24-72 hour post-arrival process.
- **Three contingency playbooks** each covering diagnosis, response, and downstream implications.
- **Specific decision criteria applied in each contingency** — not general "consider carefully" but specific "if X then Y."
- **CEO / CFO role split honoured throughout** — chapter 8's workstream split visible in each playbook.
- **Signalling-risk protocol documented** with specific outreach language and specific counter-narrative preparation.
- **Retrospective** identifying the specific vulnerabilities and the specific pre-close preparation that would have mitigated them.
- **Handoff to mod-108** noted — which term-sheet mechanics the contingencies expose as necessary.
