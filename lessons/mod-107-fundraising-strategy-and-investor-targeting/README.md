# mod-107 — Fundraising Strategy & Investor Targeting

**Running a Raise End-to-End**

**Track:** [Startup Finance & Fundraising](../../CURRICULUM.md)
**Stage:** SEED / SERIES-A
**Hours:** 28 (9 chapters + 8 exercises + planned lab + planned quiz)

## What this module installs

Valuation is the output of the raise. Every step *before* the term sheet — sizing the round against the milestone bar, choosing which investors to talk to, running a funnel that produces converging term sheets in a window, telling the story the target investor pool is underwriting to, and equipping that pool with the data room they need to close — is the raise. mod-106 priced the round. This module runs it.

The module installs the specific mechanics a CFO or fundraising-finance lead uses to execute a seed or Series-A round end-to-end alongside the founder-CEO. Specifically:

- **Round sizing against the milestone bar** — 18-24 months of runway to a defined next-round KPI bar with a milestone buffer, not a round size backed into from a target dilution target. Why an over-raised round almost always costs the founders more (immediate dilution, valuation-defence risk at the next round, hiring-pace burn errors, and the pattern-match against the next-round KPI bar).
- **Investor-target list construction** — targeting against the fund's stated thesis, its most recent portfolio adds in the sector, its cheque-size band for the target stage, and the partner-level fit + partnership-economics constraints (the fund-size × ownership-target × cheque-size math that dictates which stage of round a fund can actually lead).
- **Fundraising funnel design and running** — the target list → warm intros → first meeting → partner meeting → full partnership → term sheet ladder, with weekly cohort funnel tracking, honest disqualification of unengaged funds, and a running momentum narrative that a founder can defend when a partner asks "who else is looking?"
- **Pitch deck architecture** — the Sequoia / YC pattern (company purpose, problem, solution, why now, market size, competition, product, business model, team, financials, the raise), why the ordering is not arbitrary, and why deviating from the standard architecture without a specific reason costs conversion.
- **Data room architecture for VC diligence** — corporate documents, financials, cap table, customer contracts, product roadmap, hiring plan, references, with a clear read order and an access-controlled sharing pattern (staged access, granular NDAs, audit trails).
- **DocSend engagement benchmarks** — reading time-per-slide, drop-off pattern, and forward count as a live signal of deck-market fit; the DocSend Pitch Deck Interest Report and the Startup Fundraising Report as the benchmark anchors; iterating the deck against the signal rather than against founder taste.
- **Close choreography** — orchestrating parallel processes across investors to converge term sheets in a window; the signalling risk from unengaged funds and how to manage it; when to accept a lead's term sheet vs. shop it; how to handle a slow no vs. a fast yes; the specific mechanics of coordinating simultaneous partner meetings, on-site diligence, and reference calls.
- **The CFO's role in the raise** — model authorship and defence, data-room ownership, term-sheet analysis and red-line, board-and-legal choreography — while the CEO owns the narrative-and-relationship half. Why founder-only Series-A raises frequently cost terms and why the CFO-CEO partnership is a load-bearing input to the outcome.
- **Stage-specific fundraising dynamics** — seed vs. Series-A vs. Series-B rounds are structurally different (seed sells the founder, Series-A sells the traction, Series-B sells the operating model). Each stage rewards a different fundraising motion. Recognise which and execute it.

The module deliberately covers **execution** of the raise on top of the valuation frameworks from mod-106. Term-sheet mechanics — preferences, anti-dilution, protective provisions, board composition — are covered in [`mod-108`](../mod-108-term-sheets-and-preferred-stock-economics/). Convertible-instrument mechanics are in [`mod-105`](../mod-105-convertible-instruments/). Down-round and bridge dynamics are in [`mod-109`](../mod-109-runway-management-and-bridge-financing/).

## How this module fits

Directly depends on [`mod-102`](../mod-102-unit-economics-and-cohort-financial-modelling/) (the cohort unit-economics pack is a data-room requirement and the specific language of the traction slide), [`mod-103`](../mod-103-three-statement-model-and-driver-based-forecasting/) (the driver-based three-statement model is the data-room financial model and the source of the runway-and-milestone-bar arithmetic), [`mod-104`](../mod-104-cap-tables-and-equity-compensation/) (the cap table is the data-room primary document; the pool-shuffle math determines the founder's post-raise ownership and reads on the term sheet), [`mod-105`](../mod-105-convertible-instruments/) (the outstanding SAFEs and notes are a data-room primary document and shape the priced round the raise resolves into), and [`mod-106`](../mod-106-startup-valuation-frameworks/) (the valuation output sizes the round and shapes the investor-target list; a valuation the target pool won't pay ends the process).

Directly feeds [`mod-108`](../mod-108-term-sheets-and-preferred-stock-economics/) (the term sheets the raise produces and the negotiation against the NVCA reference), [`mod-109`](../mod-109-runway-management-and-bridge-financing/) (the milestone-bar sizing here directly determines when the next raise starts and whether a bridge is needed), and [`mod-110`](../mod-110-board-and-investor-governance-for-the-cfo/) (the investors this raise onboards become the board that governance now services).

The module *does not* teach: term-sheet mechanics (that is mod-108, and every recommendation here about "which lead's term sheet to accept" defers the actual preference / anti-dilution / protective-provision analysis to mod-108), M&A / IPO / secondary-transaction execution (that lives in [`startup-exit-curriculum`](https://github.com/ai-startup-curriculum/startup-exit-curriculum)), or the founder-narrative work of positioning and storytelling (mod-107 covers the pitch-deck *architecture*; the founder-narrative craft that fills the architecture is covered in [`founder-ceo-curriculum`](https://github.com/ai-startup-curriculum/founder-ceo-curriculum) and [`startup-product-gtm-curriculum`](https://github.com/ai-startup-curriculum/startup-product-gtm-curriculum) at the positioning-and-messaging level).
