# Resources — mod-107 Fundraising Strategy & Investor Targeting

This is the reference stack that sits behind every chapter and exercise in this module. Sources are grouped by tier of authority. Read tier-1 primary references (the practitioner canon plus the venture-industry primary sources) first; treat tier-4 practitioner content as interpretive, not authoritative.

Because fundraising-industry data — round sizes, Series-A KPI bars, DocSend engagement benchmarks, cheque-size distributions — moves materially year to year, always cite the specific report year when quoting a number in a memo or a deck. The `<!-- needs-research -->` markers in the chapters flag places where the current-year data should be re-verified against the most recent publication of the source.

## Tier 1 — Practitioner canon (venture-deal mechanics)

The books and long-form works that established the modern conventions of venture-deal execution. Every CFO or founder running a priced round should have read most of these.

- **Brad Feld and Jason Mendelson — *Venture Deals: Be Smarter Than Your Lawyer and Venture Capitalist*** — Wiley, currently in the 5th or later edition. The single most-cited practitioner reference for venture-deal mechanics: term sheets, negotiation, funnel discipline, close mechanics, and the interaction with counsel. Chapters 1, 3, 5, 7, and 8 draw on it directly. [venturedeals.com](https://www.venturedeals.com/).
- **Scott Kupor — *Secrets of Sand Hill Road: Venture Capital and How to Get It*** — Portfolio, 2019. Written by a16z's managing partner. The inside-the-VC-firm view of how partnerships are structured, how funds size cheques against fund-math, how partners underwrite deals, and how founder-CEOs should read the fund from the fund's side. Chapter 2's partnership-economics arithmetic is anchored in Kupor.
- **Mark Suster — *Both Sides of the Table*** — [bothsidesofthetable.com](https://bothsidesofthetable.com/). Long-running blog by the Upfront Ventures partner (formerly a founder). Deep archive on fundraising process, close mechanics, signalling, momentum, and the specific mechanics of running a raise. Chapters 3 and 7 reference specific Suster essays on process discipline and signalling risk.
- **Fred Wilson — *AVC*** — [avc.com](https://avc.com/). Long-running blog by the USV managing partner. Wilson's essays cover the seed-vs.-Series-A distinction, VC-side underwriting patterns, market-conditions calls, and the founder-side view of the raise. Chapter 1 (right-sizing rounds) and chapter 9 (stage-specific dynamics) draw on multiple AVC essays.
- **Paul Graham — *Startup essays*** — [paulgraham.com/articles.html](http://www.paulgraham.com/articles.html). The essays on how to raise money (*How to Raise Money*, *Fundraising Rounds*, *Fundraising Survival Guide*, *A Fundraising Survival Guide*, *The Fatal Pinch*) are the reference for the earliest-stage founder perspective. The YC deck pattern (chapter 4) is largely Graham's articulation.
- **Sam Altman — *Startup Playbook*** — [playbook.samaltman.com](https://playbook.samaltman.com/). YC / OpenAI's Altman on startup mechanics, including the fundraising-specific sections. Companion reference to Graham's essays.
- **Peter Thiel — *Zero to One*** — Crown Business, 2014. Not a fundraising-specific book but the source of much of the "why now" and market-thinking language used in pitch decks (chapter 4, slides 4 and 5).

## Tier 2 — Venture-industry primary sources

Institutional, association-published, and law-firm-published references that a CFO or fundraising lead should be able to cite by name.

- **National Venture Capital Association (NVCA)** — [nvca.org](https://nvca.org/).
  - **NVCA Model Legal Documents** — [nvca.org/model-legal-documents/](https://nvca.org/model-legal-documents/). The industry-standard model term sheet, model preferred-stock purchase agreement, and related model documents. The "NVCA reference" that every term-sheet analysis in chapter 7 and mod-108 is compared against.
  - **NVCA Venture Monitor / Yearbook** — annual data on venture activity, sector and stage breakdowns, cheque-size and round-size medians. Referenced by chapter 2 for sector- and stage-specific target-list construction.
- **PitchBook** — [pitchbook.com](https://pitchbook.com/). Subscription data provider covering VC funds, portfolio companies, and round-level data. Primary source for cheque-size ranges, fund vintage data, and portfolio-add tracking used in chapter 2's target-list construction. Free content includes summary reports and blog data.
- **Crunchbase** — [crunchbase.com](https://www.crunchbase.com/). Public database of companies, funding rounds, and investors. Primary source for round-announcement data, investor participation, and portfolio-add sequences.
- **Signal by NFX** — [signal.nfx.com](https://signal.nfx.com/). Public investor-search tool with partner-level profiles, fund thesis summaries, and portfolio-add data. Useful for chapter 2's partner-level fit hypothesis and for chapter 3's warm-intro path identification.
- **Harmonic** — [harmonic.ai](https://harmonic.ai/). Investor-search and company-signal tool. Complements Signal and Crunchbase.

## Tier 3 — Deck and data-room references

- **Sequoia Capital — *Writing a Business Plan* / pitch-deck template** — [sequoiacap.com/article/writing-a-business-plan/](https://www.sequoiacap.com/article/writing-a-business-plan/). The Sequoia pitch-deck template is the reference architecture for chapter 4's eleven-slide pattern.
- **Y Combinator — startup library / pitch guidance** — [ycombinator.com/library](https://www.ycombinator.com/library). YC's deck pattern (a compressed, founder-heavy variant of the Sequoia pattern) is the second canonical reference for chapter 4.
- **DocSend — *Startup Pitch Deck Interest Report*** — published periodically by DocSend (now part of Dropbox). Per-slide engagement benchmarks (time per slide, drop-off patterns, forwards). Chapter 6 anchors its signal-reading on this report. Current publications at [docsend.com/blog](https://www.docsend.com/blog/) and [docsend.com/insights](https://www.docsend.com/insights/).
- **DocSend — *Startup Fundraising Report*** — published periodically by DocSend. Cycle-level fundraising benchmarks (meetings to term sheet, average round duration by stage, engagement patterns for successful vs. unsuccessful raises). Referenced by chapters 3 and 6.
- **First Round Capital — *State of Startups*** — [firstround.com](https://firstround.com/) → Review. First Round's annual founder survey covers fundraising duration, dilution patterns, and CEO / CFO sentiment. Complement to DocSend's dataset.
- **First Round Review** — [review.firstround.com](https://review.firstround.com/). Long-form essays from First Round-portfolio operators and partners covering fundraising, hiring, and go-to-market. Deep archive with several essays specifically on running a raise.
- **Standard VC-diligence checklists** — Sequoia, a16z, Bessemer, and NVCA publish or reference standard diligence-request checklists. Referenced by chapter 5 and exercise 05 as the anchor for the data-room diligence-request-checklist construction.

## Tier 4 — Stage-specific benchmark sources (SaaS and beyond)

Referenced heavily in chapter 1 (next-round KPI bars) and chapter 9 (stage-transition analysis). Each publishes on a refresh cadence — cite the specific vintage.

- **Point Nine Capital — *Series-A Napkin*** — [pointnine.com](https://www.pointnine.com/) → search "Series-A Napkin." Point Nine's annually-updated summary of the Series-A KPI bar for B2B SaaS (ARR, growth rate, NRR, CAC-payback). Chapter 1 cites Point Nine as one of the anchoring sources.
- **OpenView Venture Partners — *SaaS Benchmarks Report*** — [openviewpartners.com](https://openviewpartners.com/) → Insights → SaaS Benchmarks. Annual survey of hundreds of private SaaS companies segmented by ARR band, GTM motion, and industry. Primary reference for the Series-A / Series-B bars in chapter 1 and for the stage-specific proof-artifacts in chapter 9.
- **Bessemer Venture Partners — *State of the Cloud*** — [bvp.com/atlas/state-of-the-cloud](https://www.bvp.com/atlas/state-of-the-cloud). Annual analysis with a strong late-stage and public-SaaS focus. Rule of 40, cash conversion score, and public-SaaS benchmark bands.
- **Bessemer — *Cloud 100* and *Anti-Portfolio*** — companion Bessemer content useful for chapter 9's Series-B / late-stage proof-artifacts and for the "what a fund passes on" study.
- **SaaStr — *State of SaaS* and archive** — [saastr.com](https://www.saastr.com/). Practitioner-oriented benchmarks focused on the operator community.
- **KeyBanc Capital Markets — *Annual SaaS Survey*** — distributed via [key.com](https://www.key.com/) and via industry partners. Detailed unit-economics benchmarks for private SaaS.
- **a16z — *Marketplace 100* and consumer content** — [a16z.com/marketplace-100/](https://a16z.com/marketplace-100/) and the a16z consumer blog. Reference for marketplace and consumer-specific KPI bars (chapter 1 note on consumer / marketplace benchmarks).
- **USV — *Fred Wilson's essays on marketplaces and networks*** — companion reference for consumer / marketplace stage-transition thinking.

## Reading order for this module

If you are new to the fundraising-practitioner canon, this is the order to read:

1. Paul Graham's fundraising essays (*How to Raise Money*, *Fundraising Rounds*, *Fundraising Survival Guide*) — the earliest-stage foundational framing.
2. Feld & Mendelson, *Venture Deals* — the practitioner reference for deal mechanics.
3. Kupor, *Secrets of Sand Hill Road* — the inside-the-fund view.
4. Suster's *Both Sides of the Table* — dive into the archive on fundraising process and close mechanics.
5. Wilson's *AVC* — dive into the archive on stage-specific patterns and market-conditions calls.
6. Sequoia's *Writing a Business Plan* and YC's pitch guidance — the deck-architecture references.
7. Current-vintage OpenView, Point Nine, and Bessemer benchmarks — the current-year KPI-bar data.
8. Current-vintage DocSend *Pitch Deck Interest Report* and *Startup Fundraising Report* — the engagement-signal reference.
9. NVCA model documents — read at least the model term sheet and model preferred-stock purchase agreement, cross-referenced against mod-108.
10. Cooley GO and Fenwick / WSGR / Gunderson content — the legal / market-conditions overlay.
11. A handful of specific partner podcasts / blogs for the target funds you are researching in chapter 2.

Only after that canon should you rely on secondary practitioner content (Substacks, LinkedIn essays, conference talks) — and even then, read them as translations of the canon, not as sources of truth. Every specific claim in a fundraising memo or a board pack should trace to a tier-1 through tier-3 source, with the year and the segment named.
