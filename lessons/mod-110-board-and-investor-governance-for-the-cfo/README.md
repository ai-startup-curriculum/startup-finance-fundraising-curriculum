# mod-110 — Board & Investor Governance for the CFO

**Updates, Board Packs, Board Meetings, and the CFO-CEO Partnership that Sits Behind Them**

**Track:** [Startup Finance & Fundraising](../../CURRICULUM.md)
**Stage:** SERIES-A / SERIES-B / SERIES-C
**Hours:** 22 (8 chapters + 7 exercises + planned lab + planned quiz)

## What this module installs

Every previous module in this track produced an artifact — accrual-basis financials, cohort unit-economics, a driver-based model, a defensible cap table, a valuation, a fundraise motion, a negotiated term sheet, a runway plan under a bridge. This module installs the **standing communication channel** those artifacts land in: the monthly investor update, the quarterly board pack, the board meeting itself, the decision memos and consents that come out of it, the committee structures that grow up around it, and the CFO-CEO partnership that authors most of it.

Governance is where the CFO's technical work becomes decision-grade. A model that never appears in a board pack changes nothing. A KPI dashboard that lives in a Notion page but does not reconcile to the same actuals as the financials produces two versions of the truth and destroys board trust. A "walk the board through the pack" meeting where every slide is presented live to a board that has not read it produces zero governance value and consumes three hours of the most expensive calendar time the company has access to. The CFO who does not own this channel gets to complain about the board's decisions; the CFO who owns it gets to shape them.

Specifically:

- **The monthly investor update** — a concise one-page-plus-charts artifact that names the top KPI, states the specific ask (intros, hires, feedback), calls out the risks, and closes with the qualitative wins and losses in a voice a busy investor will actually read. Not a mini board pack. Not a marketing document. A signal channel.
- **The quarterly board pack** — KPI dashboard reconciled to the financial model, financials in an actuals-vs.-budget-vs.-prior-quarter frame, a strategic-topic deep-dive (usually one per meeting), risks / decisions / consents, and the CEO letter — with the pack sent 72 hours pre-meeting so the board arrives read-in. The 72-hour rule is not decorum; it is the mechanic that lets the meeting spend its time on discussion rather than presentation.
- **The board meeting itself** — designed as pre-read → 30-minute KPI check-in → 60-minute strategic deep-dive → 30-minute risks / decisions / consents → executive session, on the assumption that the pack has been read. The "walk through the pack" meeting is a governance-failure pattern; this chapter names it and installs the alternative.
- **Consents and action items** — the mechanics of written consent for board decisions (Delaware DGCL §141(f) and the equivalent in the company's state of incorporation), the tracking of consent-required items vs. informational, and the running action-item register that the CFO owns between meetings.
- **Decision memos** — the CFO-drafted structured memo for a board-decision item (fundraise, major hire, capital expenditure, M&A, restructuring), in the context / decision-required / options / recommendation / risks pattern, and the CEO-CFO pre-alignment work behind it.
- **Board committees** — the audit committee (needed by Series-B for a pre-IPO track, earlier for regulated sectors), the compensation committee (needed once ESOP grants exceed leadership-only), the transaction committee (M&A / financing), their charters, cadence, and independent-director requirements.
- **The CFO-CEO partnership across the fundraising cycle** — pre-raise strategic alignment (memo shape, milestone bar, investor targeting), during-raise choreography (who owns which conversation), post-raise operating alignment (board-pack refresh against the new plan) — and the specific reason a CEO-only raise at Series-A frequently costs terms because the model and diligence require CFO ownership.
- **Difficult board conversations** — a miss to plan, the departure of a senior hire, a customer concentration risk, a founder disagreement — with the frame that governance is not adversarial and the CFO's role is honest-signal delivery, not spin.

This module owns the **communication and governance discipline** on an ongoing-company basis. It does not teach the board *composition* negotiation (that is [mod-108 chapter 7](../mod-108-term-sheets-and-preferred-stock-economics/07-board-composition-and-independent-director-selection.md)) or the deep legal-mechanics of fiduciary duty and corporate formalities (those belong in the operations / governance sideways pillar and in specialist legal counsel). It teaches the CFO how to run the standing channel between the board, the investors, and the operating company.
