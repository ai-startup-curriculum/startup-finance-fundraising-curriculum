# Exercise 06 — Audit and Compensation Committee Charter Drill

**Estimated time:** ~4 hours
**Prerequisites:** Chapter 6 (board committees — audit, compensation, and transaction). Familiarity with the SEC Regulation S-K Item 407(d)(5) financial-expert definition, and with NYSE Corporate Governance Standards §303A.06-07 / Nasdaq Rule 5605(c)-(d), is expected; both are the reference for the pre-IPO audit-committee and compensation-committee charter drafting.

## Problem statement

For a specified Series-B company on a pre-IPO track, draft the audit-committee charter and the compensation-committee charter, define the meeting cadence for each, and produce the independent-director sourcing plan for the seats each committee requires. Both charters must be adopted-by-the-full-board-ready — that is, in the format a full board would resolve to adopt via a §141(f) written consent (exercise 04) — and must include the specific SEC / stock-exchange references that pre-IPO companies typically build against so the transition to public-company status a few years later is smooth.

The exercise trains two related disciplines: the governance-structure design work that transitions a company from "single unified board" to "board plus committees" (a Series-B / Series-C threshold), and the independent-director sourcing work that populates the committees. Both disciplines are load-bearing at the pre-IPO stage; a company that arrives at the S-1 window without functioning committees or without the required independent-director count adds 6-12 months to its IPO calendar, at material cost.

## Scenario — the pre-IPO track Series-B company

You are the CFO of a Series-B B2B SaaS company at $18M ARR, 100 employees, closed a $40M Series-B 10 months ago at a $220M post-money. The company has a plausible IPO path in 3-4 years; the CEO and the board have agreed to prepare governance for pre-IPO readiness starting now.

**Current board.** Six directors: two founder-CEO / co-founder seats (the CEO and the CTO), one Series-A lead director, one Series-B lead director, one Series-A-appointed independent director (a former public-company CFO), one Series-B-appointed independent director (a former product / operator). No committees currently formed. Corporate counsel is a large national firm with a strong pre-IPO practice.

**The specific ask.** The board has voted at the most recent quarterly meeting to form both an audit committee and a compensation committee, effective at the next quarterly meeting (90 days out). The CFO is drafting the charters and the sourcing plan. The board has authorised the addition of one more independent-director seat if the CFO's sourcing plan justifies it.

**Sector context.** SaaS (not regulated). No specific-industry audit-committee requirements beyond the general pre-IPO track. Compensation-committee formation is triggered because ESOP grants have expanded past leadership-only to include a wider senior-manager grant cohort, and the board has concluded that grants should not be approved by the full board in that volume.

**Transaction-committee context.** Not applicable in this exercise (no active M&A or financing transaction requires the transaction-committee mechanic yet). The extensions section covers the transaction-committee variant if you want to add it.

## Requirements

Produce a submission directory containing the following.

### Part A — the audit-committee charter

1. **The audit-committee charter** (`01-audit-committee-charter.md`). Four-to-six pages. Full charter in the format that the full board will adopt by resolution. Sections (per chapter 6):
   - **Purpose.** Oversight of financial reporting, internal controls, and the outside auditor.
   - **Composition.** Number of members (three); requirement that all members be independent (define independence explicitly, referencing the SEC / NYSE / Nasdaq definitions); requirement that at least one member qualify as an "audit committee financial expert" (define per SEC Regulation S-K Item 407(d)(5)); appointment process.
   - **Authority and responsibilities.** Include the specific responsibilities: selection and engagement of the outside auditor; approval of audit fees; approval of non-audit services above a specified threshold (name the threshold); review of quarterly and annual financial statements; oversight of internal controls; oversight of financial-reporting risk; oversight of any whistleblower or complaint procedures; oversight of related-party transactions.
   - **Meetings.** Frequency (quarterly, with the option of more frequent as needed); requirement to meet privately with the outside auditor at each meeting; requirement to meet privately with the CFO at each meeting; requirement to meet in executive session (without management present) at each meeting.
   - **Reporting.** The committee's reporting obligation to the full board (typically a report at each quarterly full-board meeting).
   - **Committee resources.** Authority to engage independent legal, accounting, or other advisors at the company's expense.
   - **Annual charter review.** Requirement to review and reaffirm the charter annually.
   - **Adoption clause and effective date.**

2. **The audit-committee cadence and workplan** (`02-audit-cadence.md`). One-to-two pages. The specific meeting cadence:
   - Quarterly full committee meetings, coincident with the board pack cycle. State whether the audit committee meets the day before the full-board meeting (a common pattern) or on a separate schedule.
   - Specific annual-cycle workplan: audit-plan approval at the year-open meeting; interim-financial-statement review at each quarterly meeting; annual-financial-statement review at the year-close meeting; audit-committee-only executive-session cadence.
   - Between-meeting activity: standing dialogue between the committee chair and the CFO / outside auditor; any consents circulated between meetings.
   - Reference to the audit-committee-only packet the CFO prepares (referenced in exercise 02 extension).

3. **The audit-committee-financial-expert sourcing plan** (`03-audit-financial-expert-sourcing.md`). One-to-two pages. Includes:
   - The specific S-K Item 407(d)(5) qualifying criteria and a compatibility check against the two existing independent directors: does the Series-A-appointed independent (former public-company CFO) qualify? If yes, name the specific qualifying experience. If no, name the gap.
   - If the existing independents do not qualify, the specific sourcing plan for a new independent director who does: profile requirements, sourcing channels (Bolster, Extend, other board-marketplace platforms, executive-search firms specialising in board placement, the existing board members' networks), diligence process, compensation structure (typical board-only compensation for early-stage independents is options — reference specific benchmark data or mark `needs-research`), and expected timeline to seat.
   - Backup plan if the primary sourcing plan does not produce a candidate in the 90-day window.

### Part B — the compensation-committee charter

4. **The compensation-committee charter** (`04-compensation-committee-charter.md`). Three-to-five pages. Full charter, similar structure to the audit-committee charter:
   - **Purpose.** Oversight of executive compensation, stock plan administration, and compensation-related governance.
   - **Composition.** Number of members (two-to-three); independence requirement (define per the applicable NYSE / Nasdaq compensation-committee-specific definition, which differs from the audit-committee definition); appointment process. Address whether an investor-affiliated director (a Series-B lead's partner) can serve on the compensation committee at a pre-IPO stage (chapter 6 discusses this).
   - **Authority and responsibilities.** All executive-officer compensation actions; all stock-plan grants above a specified threshold (name the threshold — e.g., grants to VP-and-above); annual comp-benchmark review; recommendation to the full board on the CEO's compensation; oversight of the ESOP / stock plan.
   - **Meetings.** Cadence (quarterly minimum, with additional meetings as needed for grant approvals). Executive-session requirement (without any executive present when discussing CEO compensation).
   - **Reporting.** To the full board.
   - **Committee resources.** Authority to engage independent compensation consultants (name a specific benchmark-data source such as Radford or Pave, and reference the specific benchmark-data reports the committee will use).
   - **Annual charter review.**
   - **Adoption clause.**

5. **The compensation-committee cadence and workplan** (`05-comp-cadence.md`). One page. Similar to the audit cadence document but for compensation-committee-specific rhythm:
   - Quarterly meetings coincident with the board pack cycle.
   - Annual comp-cycle events: benchmark-refresh (Q1 or Q2, using the specific benchmark-data source named in the charter); executive-comp-review (Q4, in advance of the year-open compensation decisions); ESOP top-up review (typically Q4 or Q1, in advance of the annual grant cycle).
   - Between-meeting activity: individual grant approvals below the full-board threshold; specific new-hire-compensation approvals for senior positions.

6. **The compensation-committee independent-director-source plan** (`06-comp-committee-sourcing.md`). One page. Similar to the audit-committee sourcing plan:
   - Compatibility check of existing independent directors against the compensation-committee independence definition (which may differ from the audit-committee definition — check specifically).
   - If a new independent is needed, the sourcing plan: profile requirements (compensation-committee members typically have HR / people-function or comp-consulting backgrounds), sourcing channels, diligence, compensation, timeline.
   - If the same new-independent-director candidate can serve on both the audit and compensation committees, note that and adjust the audit-committee sourcing plan accordingly. Independent directors serving on multiple committees is common at the pre-IPO stage and reduces the number of independent-director seats to fill.

### Part C — the full-board adoption pack

7. **The full-board adoption resolution pack** (`07-adoption-resolutions.md`). Two pages. The specific board resolutions to be adopted at the next quarterly meeting or by DGCL §141(f) written consent (per exercise 04):
   - Resolution 1: adopting the audit-committee charter and appointing the initial audit-committee members.
   - Resolution 2: adopting the compensation-committee charter and appointing the initial compensation-committee members.
   - Resolution 3: authorising the CFO to engage an independent-director search firm (if needed) and authorising the search-firm fees within a stated budget.
   - Resolution 4: delegating specific compensation-committee-scope authority per the charter, and specifying the ratification process for grants approved by the compensation committee before the next full-board meeting.

8. **The CFO transition plan** (`08-cfo-transition-plan.md`). One-to-two pages. The CFO's operational transition plan for the shift from "single unified board" governance to "board plus two committees" governance. Includes:
   - The specific changes to the quarterly board-pack architecture (audit-committee packet as a pre-board-meeting document; compensation-committee packet on the same cycle).
   - The specific changes to the CFO's own calendar (audit-committee prep; compensation-committee prep; committee-chair dialogue between meetings).
   - The specific changes to the finance-team responsibilities (a controller-level or director-of-finance responsible for the audit-committee support; a people-team partnership responsible for the compensation-committee support).
   - The specific changes to the corporate-record-keeping (committee minutes; committee written consents; annual charter-review calendar).
   - The specific escalation paths for a committee-only decision that needs full-board ratification.

## Starter guidance

- **Read chapter 6 in full before drafting.** The committee-formation triggers, the independent-director definitions, and the specific charter contents are all specified there. Attempting the charters without a re-read produces a boilerplate charter that will not pass a serious pre-IPO governance review.
- **Reference the primary sources for the independence definitions.** The audit-committee independence definition (SEC / stock-exchange rules), the audit-committee-financial-expert definition (SEC Regulation S-K Item 407(d)(5)), and the compensation-committee-specific independence definition (NYSE / Nasdaq stock-exchange rules) are all specific and are what pre-IPO companies build against. Cite the specific sections in the charter or the sourcing plans; do not paraphrase.
- **The audit committee is a real check on management and on major stockholders.** Investor-affiliated directors (a Series-B lead's partner) are typically not audit-committee-independent. If the current board has no unaffiliated independents who qualify, the sourcing plan is not optional — it is the constraint that determines whether the committee can be formed at all in the current quarter.
- **The financial-expert requirement is specific.** Not "someone who understands finance" — a specific S-K definition. A former CFO of a public company, a former audit partner at a Big Four firm, a former controller of a public company, or a senior finance academic with the requisite experience. Match the sourcing plan to the specific criteria.
- **The compensation-committee independence definition differs from the audit-committee definition.** In particular, the NYSE and Nasdaq have specific "compensation-committee independence" tests that are stricter than the general "independent director" test — they include a specific check on any consulting or advisory fees paid to the director, and a specific check on affiliations with major stockholders. Reference the specific test in the charter.
- **The cadence documents are real workplans.** Not calendar placeholders. The specific-annual-cycle events (audit-plan approval, benchmark refresh, executive-comp review, ESOP top-up review) are what turn a charter into a functioning committee.
- **Independent-director sourcing takes 3-6 months.** The 90-day timeline for this exercise is tight; the sourcing plan should be honest about whether the committee can be seated with existing directors in the near term and with a new independent director recruited over the next 6 months.
- **The full-board adoption pack integrates with exercise 04.** The specific resolutions are the format that would appear in a §141(f) written consent or in a meeting-vote resolution.
- **The CFO transition plan is where the real operational shift happens.** A charter without a CFO transition plan is a charter that will exist on paper but not function. Draft the transition plan honestly, including the pieces the CFO cannot do alone.
- **Cite benchmark-compensation data specifically.** Radford, Pave, Option Impact, Compensia — reference the specific report and vintage. If you cite an early-stage-independent-director option-grant benchmark, cite the specific data or mark `<!-- needs-research: verify current early-stage independent-director-grant benchmark -->`.

## Acceptance criteria

- **All eight deliverables present.** Audit charter, audit cadence, audit sourcing, compensation charter, compensation cadence, compensation sourcing, full-board adoption resolutions, CFO transition plan.
- **Each charter includes all specified sections.** Purpose, composition (with specific independence definition cited to primary source), authority and responsibilities, meetings, reporting, resources, annual review, adoption.
- **The audit charter cites SEC / NYSE / Nasdaq references specifically** — Regulation S-K Item 407(d)(5), NYSE §303A.06-07, Nasdaq 5605(c).
- **The compensation charter cites the compensation-committee-specific independence rules** and addresses the investor-affiliated-director question explicitly.
- **The cadence documents are real workplans** with specific annual-cycle events, not just meeting-frequency statements.
- **The independent-director sourcing plans include a specific compatibility check** of the existing independents against the committee-specific independence criteria.
- **The full-board adoption resolution pack is in the format of §141(f) resolutions** and would be filed with the meeting minutes or as a between-meeting written consent.
- **The CFO transition plan addresses the operational shift** — pack changes, calendar changes, finance-team-responsibility changes, corporate-record-keeping changes.
- **No fabricated legal or benchmark references.** Every citation is either to a specific primary source or marked `needs-research`.

## Extensions (optional)

- **The transaction-committee variant.** Add a transaction-committee charter for a scenario where the same company is entering a specific M&A conversation (an inbound acquisition inquiry from a specific strategic acquirer). Cover the ad-hoc charter (transaction-specific scope), the specific composition (two independents plus the CEO, with the CFO as staff), the interaction with the full board (advisory / recommendation vs. binding authority), the specific reporting cadence, and the sunset (charter dissolves at transaction close or termination).
- **The regulated-sector variant.** Rewrite the audit-committee charter for a fintech company at Series-A that faces specific bank-regulatory oversight (a specific state's money-transmitter licence, or a specific banking-partner-imposed audit requirement). Add the regulatory-oversight responsibilities and the specific regulator-facing communication cadence. Trains the sector-specific adjustment of the reference charter.
- **The audit-committee-first-meeting playbook.** For the first quarterly meeting of the new audit committee, draft the specific pre-meeting materials, the committee-only-with-outside-auditor session agenda, the specific first-meeting decisions (auditor engagement or ratification, audit-plan approval, initial internal-controls scoping), and the report-back to the full board.
- **The comp-committee benchmark-refresh workplan.** For the annual comp-benchmark cycle, draft the specific workplan: which comp-data source is engaged (Radford, Pave, Option Impact, Compensia — cite specifics or mark `needs-research`), which peer set is used (name the specific peer companies or the peer-set methodology), which internal roles are benchmarked (executive-officers only vs. all VP-and-above), and the specific committee-approval steps for the resulting compensation actions.
- **The IPO-track governance-timeline memo.** For the pre-IPO path 3-4 years out, draft the CFO's governance-readiness timeline. Names the specific governance-readiness milestones by year (audit-committee functioning at pre-IPO standard for 12-18 months before S-1; compensation-committee functioning; specific additional independent-director recruitment to reach the majority-independent-board standard required for public status; specific charter-refresh events to move from pre-IPO to public-company standard). Trains the multi-year governance-planning discipline.
- **The retrospective view.** Assume it is 24 months later. The audit committee has been functioning; the compensation committee has been functioning. Draft the CFO's honest retrospective on which parts of the charters and workplans held up in practice and which parts needed to be adjusted. Trains the "the first draft of the charter is not the final draft" discipline.
