# Exercise 07 — CFO-CEO Partnership Role-Play

**Estimated time:** ~4 hours
**Prerequisites:** Chapter 7 (the CFO-CEO partnership across the fundraising cycle). Chapters 5 (decision memos) and 8 (difficult board conversations) are useful cross-references. Familiarity with mod-107 (fundraising strategy) and mod-108 (term sheets) is expected.

## Problem statement

Role-play the pre-raise, during-raise, and post-raise CFO-CEO conversations across a Series-B raise. Produce three specific artefacts — the pre-raise alignment memo, the during-raise choreography script, and the post-raise plan-reset memo — that capture the conversation content, the specific alignment work, the specific division of responsibilities, and the specific handoffs. The role-play surfaces the specific moments where the CFO-CEO partnership either holds (a coordinated raise that closes on strong terms) or fractures (a CEO-only-shaped raise that costs terms), and installs the CFO's specific script for each moment.

The exercise trains the partnership discipline that most differentiates a CFO who is a real partner to the CEO on the raise from a CFO who is a support function. Chapter 7's central claim is that CEO-only raises at Series-A and later frequently cost 10-25% on either valuation or size; the mechanic that captures that value is not any single tool but the specific conversation cadence and the specific artefact set the partnership produces. This exercise builds the cadence and the artefact set.

## Scenario — the Series-B raise

You are the CFO of a B2B SaaS company at $8M ARR, growing 8% MoM, 60 employees, cash on hand $12M with $900K monthly burn (13 months of runway). The company closed a $12M Series-A 18 months ago at a $50M post-money. Existing board: two founder-CEO / co-founder seats, one Series-A lead director, one seed-era independent director.

**The raise.** The company is opening a Series-B raise in Q4 (approximately 90 days out from the current moment). Target round size $30-40M at a post-money in the $180-280M range depending on how the milestone bar (aiming for $10M ARR at raise-open with 100%+ NRR) and the current market conditions land. Existing Series-A lead is expected to participate but not to lead; the raise is targeting a new institutional lead (a tier-1 fund with strong Series-B track record).

**The CFO's specific starting position.** The CFO joined the company 4 months ago after a fractional-CFO period that started 8 months ago. The CFO has led the last two board packs. The board and the existing lead investor know the CFO but the new lead-investor set does not. The CFO's specific concerns entering the raise:

- The financial model has been rebuilt but has not been stress-tested against institutional-diligence questions.
- The data room is partially built but was on the fractional-CFO's calendar and needs a controller-level pass to be fully diligence-ready.
- The CEO has a strong network with the target-lead-investor set and a strong track record of running raise conversations from the previous seed and Series-A, but has not yet operated a raise where the CFO is a co-lead rather than support.
- The CEO's initial framing is "you handle the model and the data room; I'll handle the investor conversations." The CFO believes this framing is the CEO-only pattern chapter 7 warns against and needs to be re-negotiated before the raise opens.

## Requirements

Produce a submission directory containing the following.

### Part A — pre-raise (3-6 months out)

1. **The raise-strategy alignment memo** (`01-raise-strategy.md`). Three-to-five pages. The CFO-drafted memo (with CEO input) that captures the aligned raise strategy. Chapter-7 structure:
   - Target round size and milestone bar (a milestone bar, not just a valuation target).
   - Target investor set (per mod-107 chapter 2): named leads, named co-investors, named backup lead options.
   - Target close timing and the runway position at that close.
   - Fallback options if the raise does not close on the planned timing (references mod-109 chapter 1).
   - The specific milestones the company has to hit in the pre-raise window (ARR trajectory, key hires, product milestones, customer wins).
   - A one-page summary of the specific division of responsibilities between the CEO and the CFO during the raise (previewing the choreography script in Part B).

2. **The pre-raise partnership re-negotiation script** (`02-partnership-renegotiation-script.md`). Two-to-three pages. The CFO's specific script for the conversation with the CEO where the CFO surfaces the CEO-only-pattern concern and re-negotiates the division of responsibilities. Includes:
   - The specific opening the CFO uses ("I want to lay out how I think we should split responsibilities during the raise, because the way we've been talking about it is closer to a support-function pattern than a real co-lead pattern").
   - The specific data or examples the CFO references (chapter 7's specific reasons the CEO-only pattern costs terms; specific portfolio-company examples if the CFO has them).
   - The specific concessions the CFO is prepared to make ("I'm not asking to lead the founder-story conversations — you're better at those. I'm asking to be in the room for the CFO-shaped diligence conversations and to co-lead the term-sheet negotiation").
   - The specific concrete alignment output the conversation is trying to produce (the choreography script in Part B).
   - The specific fallback if the CEO resists ("if we cannot align on this now, my recommendation is that we defer the raise open by 4-6 weeks so we can work through the alignment before we go to market").

3. **The pre-raise readiness checklist** (`03-readiness-checklist.md`). One-to-two pages. The specific checklist of pre-raise work the CFO owns:
   - Model refresh (historicals cleaned, forecast rebuilt, scenario / sensitivity heatmap ready, cash / runway model complete). Named specific due dates.
   - Data room build (standard sections populated per mod-107 chapter 5; document-naming conventions enforced). Named specific due dates.
   - KPI-definition lock (per chapter 2). Named specific due dates.
   - Pitch-deck data reconciliation (every chart, metric, and projection reconciles to the model). Named specific due dates.
   - Diligence-Q&A prep (the CFO's anticipated top 30-50 diligence questions with prepared responses). Named specific due dates.
   - Reference lists (customer references, employee references, prior-investor references) prepared per mod-107 chapter 4.

### Part B — during-raise (the raise window itself)

4. **The choreography script** (`04-choreography-script.md`). Three-to-five pages. The specific conversation-by-conversation script for how the CFO and CEO divide the raise's investor interactions. Includes:
   - **First-meeting script.** Who takes the meeting (CEO leads, CFO in the room; alternate scenarios: CFO leads a specific CFO-shaped first meeting with a lead's fund CFO or with an associate). Who covers which content (CEO: founder story, market thesis, product; CFO: model overview, KPI trajectory, financial-diligence readiness). Handoff conventions in-meeting.
   - **Partner-meeting script.** Who is in the room (both, typically; extend if the CFO's presence adds specific value to a particular partner-meeting-set format). Who owns the response to specific expected question types (dilution / cap-table questions → CFO; product-strategy questions → CEO; hiring-plan questions → both).
   - **Follow-up-diligence script.** Who owns each specific diligence workstream (financial diligence → CFO; commercial diligence → CEO with CFO support on the customer-financials cut; technical diligence → CEO / CTO with CFO covering financial-technology / infrastructure-cost questions; legal diligence → outside counsel with CFO as day-to-day owner).
   - **Term-sheet-conversation script.** Who takes the initial term-sheet-received call (CEO with CFO in the room). Who leads the counter-terms negotiation (co-led per chapter 7; the CFO owns the economic-and-governance clauses of mod-108; the CEO owns the valuation-and-board-composition clauses; both align on any protective-provisions negotiation).
   - **Reference-call script.** Who reaches out to whom (CEO: prior investor references, board references; CFO: prior CFO / finance-team references, prior audit / accounting references).
   - **The specific "who covers what if the other is unavailable" contingency.** A raise-window conflict (a customer emergency, an illness, a personal event) will happen; the choreography includes the specific handoff pattern.

5. **The weekly investor-update-during-raise** (`05-raise-window-weekly-update.md`). One-to-two pages. A sample of the raise-window weekly update to existing investors (per chapter 1's raise-exception cadence). Covers: current diligence status, meetings in progress, term-sheet activity if any, milestones hit against the pre-raise readiness checklist, any risks the raise is surfacing. Written in the same signal voice as the monthly update. Distributed to the existing-investor set (including the Series-A lead who is the pro-rata participant), plus the board.

6. **The specific-question rehearsal script** (`06-diligence-rehearsal.md`). Two-to-three pages. Ten-to-fifteen specific institutional-diligence questions with the specific CFO / CEO responses prepared. Split the questions by owner (CFO-answered vs. CEO-answered vs. jointly-answered). Include the tough questions that specifically differentiate the CFO's presence: waterfall math under a $200M exit at the current preference stack (mod-108 chapter 3); gross retention vs. net retention broken out by cohort (mod-102); the specific ROI calculation on the last three material hires; the specific sensitivity of the milestone-bar KPI to a modest slowdown; the specific quality-of-earnings adjustments (accounting-policy elections, deferred-revenue balance behaviour, one-time-vs.-recurring cost mix).

### Part C — post-raise (post-close operational alignment)

7. **The post-raise plan-reset memo** (`07-plan-reset.md`). Three-to-five pages. The CFO-drafted memo that captures the operating plan reset after the Series-B closes. Chapter-7-and-5 structure:
   - Context: the raise closed at [specific size and valuation]. New runway position. New board composition (with the new lead's director added).
   - Decision required: board affirmation of the operating plan reset — the specific KPI targets for the next 4-8 quarters, the specific hiring plan, the specific capex plan, the specific runway trajectory to the next raise.
   - Options: three plans at different growth-vs.-burn balances (aggressive growth, moderate growth, conservative growth). Each with the specific KPI trajectory, hiring plan, cash position, and Series-C-readiness projection.
   - Recommendation: the specific chosen plan with the specific milestones and the specific conditions under which the plan would shift.
   - Risks: three-to-five risks with mitigation.

8. **The first-board-pack-post-Series-B briefing** (`08-first-post-raise-pack-briefing.md`). One-to-two pages. The CFO's specific briefing note for the first board pack after the raise closes:
   - The new-director onboarding plan: what the new director will be sent in advance, what onboarding session (if any) precedes the first meeting, who from the operating team meets with the new director for a first-meeting-context conversation.
   - The KPI-dashboard adjustment: any KPI-definition changes to reflect the new plan (per chapter 2's KPI-definition-table discipline; changes require a memo and board approval before they take effect).
   - The strategic-deep-dive selection: what the first-post-raise strategic deep-dive is (typically the operating-plan-reset itself, referring the board back to the plan-reset memo from Part C requirement 7).
   - The pre-read call cadence: the specific pre-read calls the CFO plans with the new director, the existing directors, and any observers.

9. **The retrospective / lessons-learned memo** (`09-partnership-retrospective.md`). One page. Written 30-60 days after the raise closes. The CFO's honest retrospective on the CEO-CFO partnership during the raise: what held up (specific examples), what fractured (specific examples), what the CFO would do differently next time. Not for external circulation; the CFO's own artefact. Also a check on the pre-raise partnership re-negotiation (Part A requirement 2) — did the re-negotiated split actually hold in practice.

## Starter guidance

- **Read chapter 7 in full before drafting.** The specific reasons CEO-only raises cost terms, the specific pre-raise / during-raise / post-raise disciplines, and the specific choreography patterns are all there. Attempting the exercise without a re-read produces artefacts that read as generic advice rather than the specific chapter-7 pattern.
- **The pre-raise partnership re-negotiation script is the hardest artefact.** Most first-time CFOs skip this conversation entirely and end up in the CEO-only pattern by default. Draft the script honestly, including the specific concessions the CFO is willing to make and the specific fallback if the CEO resists.
- **The choreography script is not "the CEO does the story stuff, the CFO does the number stuff."** That framing is the CEO-only pattern in disguise. The chapter-7 choreography is more specific: the CFO is in the room for the diligence conversations, the CFO co-leads the term-sheet negotiation, and the CFO covers specific expected-question types in every investor meeting. Draft accordingly.
- **The weekly-update-during-raise is a specific chapter-1 exception.** Do not skip it. Even during an intense raise window, the existing-investor set needs the update on a weekly cadence — both because they are supporting the pro-rata process and because a raise-window silence produces the "we haven't heard from you in a while" call at the worst possible time.
- **The specific-question rehearsal is where the CFO's presence adds the most value in the raise.** Every question in the list is a question the CFO answers differently than the CEO would. Draft the responses specifically; do not summarise as "the CFO can answer this."
- **The post-raise plan reset happens fast.** Chapter 7 specifies that the first board pack after the raise closes needs to reflect the new plan — the CFO's calendar in the 4-6 weeks after close is dense with the plan reset, the KPI-dashboard adjustment, and the new-director onboarding.
- **The retrospective is honest.** The CEO-CFO partnership during a raise is stressful; some things fracture. Naming the specific fractures in the retrospective is what turns the raise experience into a compound-improvement input for the next raise.
- **No fabricated market data.** If the raise strategy memo references current-market Series-B valuation medians, cite specific Fenwick / WSGR / Carta reports and vintages, or mark `<!-- needs-research: ... -->`.

## Acceptance criteria

- **All nine deliverables present.** Raise-strategy memo, partnership re-negotiation script, readiness checklist, choreography script, weekly-update sample, diligence rehearsal, post-raise plan reset, first-post-raise pack briefing, retrospective.
- **The raise-strategy memo names the milestone bar** (not just a valuation target), the target investor set with specific names or targeting criteria, the target close timing with specific runway position at close, and the specific fallback options.
- **The partnership re-negotiation script names the specific CEO-only pattern concern**, the specific data or examples the CFO uses, the specific concessions the CFO is prepared to make, and the specific fallback if the CEO resists.
- **The choreography script is specific by conversation type** (first-meeting, partner-meeting, follow-up-diligence, term-sheet-conversation, reference-call, contingency handoff), not generic.
- **The weekly-update sample is in the chapter-1 signal voice** and covers the raise-window-specific content.
- **The diligence rehearsal includes at least 10 specific questions** with prepared responses split by owner. At least half the questions require CFO-specific knowledge to answer well.
- **The post-raise plan reset is in the chapter-5 decision-memo structure** with three directly-comparable plan options.
- **The first-post-raise pack briefing addresses new-director onboarding, KPI-dashboard adjustment, strategic-deep-dive selection, and the pre-read call cadence.**
- **The retrospective is honest** — it names at least one specific thing that fractured, not just a list of things that held up.
- **No fabricated market data.** All benchmark references cite specific sources with vintages or are flagged `needs-research`.

## Extensions (optional)

- **The failed-raise variant.** Assume the raise does not close on the planned timing — the target lead passes after four weeks of diligence, and the next-best-lead conversation stalls. Rewrite the raise-strategy memo and the plan-reset memo for the failed-raise scenario: pursue an insider extension (per mod-109 chapter 2), extend the runway operationally, and re-open the raise 4-6 months later. Draft the specific CEO-CFO conversation about the pivot and the specific board communication (per chapter 8's honest-signal frame).
- **The CFO-CEO disagreement during the raise.** Assume mid-raise, a specific decision surfaces where the CEO and CFO disagree — e.g., the CEO wants to accept a term-sheet with unfavourable protective provisions in exchange for a higher headline valuation, and the CFO recommends countering hard on the protective provisions even at the risk of losing the lead. Draft the specific pre-alignment conversation, the specific board-consultation approach if the disagreement cannot be resolved between the two, and the specific outcome. Trains the disagreement-under-pressure discipline.
- **The CFO-hired-during-the-raise variant.** Redraft the pre-raise artefacts for a scenario where the CFO is hired 30 days into an active raise (the prior CFO departed; the CEO has been running the raise alone). The CFO's specific first-30-day plan, the specific triage decisions (what can still be salvaged, what has to be re-worked, whether the raise timing has to shift), and the specific CEO-CFO alignment conversation on the compressed timeline.
- **The multi-lead-competing-term-sheet variant.** Assume two leads come back with term sheets within a week of each other. Add the specific CEO-CFO conversation on how to compare the two term sheets (per mod-108 exercise 01 and chapter 8), how to negotiate one against the other, and how to reach the final-lead decision.
- **The fractional-CFO variant.** Redraft the choreography script and the readiness checklist for a scenario where the CFO is fractional (2-3 days per week) rather than full-time. Names the specific responsibilities that can and cannot be handled at the fractional cadence, the specific augmentation the CFO needs (a controller-level finance director, an outside diligence-prep advisor), and the specific timing when the company must convert to a full-time CFO.
- **The CFO / lead-investor pre-alignment.** In parallel with the CFO-CEO alignment, the CFO runs a specific pre-alignment with the Series-A lead's director (per chapter 5 and chapter 7). Draft the specific script for that conversation: what the CFO shares about the raise strategy, what the CFO asks for (introductions, pro-rata commitment signal, term-sheet-preview from the lead's own experience). Trains the multi-counterpart alignment discipline.
