# Resources — mod-110 Board & Investor Governance for the CFO

This is the reference stack that sits behind every chapter and exercise in this module. Sources are grouped by tier of authority. The primary-source tier is Delaware corporate law (specifically DGCL §141 and §228), the NVCA Model Legal Documents, and the SEC / stock-exchange audit-committee and compensation-committee standards; every consent-mechanics claim and every committee-charter reference in chapters 4 and 6 traces to those documents. The practitioner-canon tier is Brad Feld's *Startup Boards*, David Sacks's board-meeting essay, Mark Suster's board archive on *Both Sides of the Table*, and Fred Wilson's *AVC* governance essays; every meeting-design and update-authoring pattern in chapters 1-3 traces to that canon.

Because the specific corporate-formality mechanics of consent and committee formation depend on the company's state of incorporation and, at the pre-IPO stage, on the specific stock-exchange listing standards the company is preparing for, always confirm the specific reference against the current vintage of the primary source before drafting into a live consent or charter. The `<!-- needs-research -->` markers in the chapters flag places where the current-vintage data or the specific regulatory reference should be re-verified.

## Tier 1 — Primary legal and regulatory sources

The load-bearing statutory and regulatory tier. Read these first.

- **Delaware General Corporation Law (DGCL)** — [Title 8 of the Delaware Code](https://delcode.delaware.gov/title8/), specifically:
  - **DGCL §141 — Board of Directors; powers** — [delcode.delaware.gov/title8/c001/sc04/index.html#141](https://delcode.delaware.gov/title8/c001/sc04/index.html#141). §141(f) is the specific board-written-consent mechanic (in lieu of a meeting; unanimous; written; effective when the last director signs). Directly referenced in chapter 4 and exercise 04.
  - **DGCL §144 — Interested directors; quorum** — the specific mechanic for approving related-party transactions with independent-director approval. Referenced in chapter 4's related-party discussion and exercise 04's extension.
  - **DGCL §228 — Consent of stockholders in lieu of meeting** — the specific stockholder-written-consent mechanic (majority or higher per the charter; requires prompt notice to non-signing stockholders per §228(e)). Referenced in chapter 4 and exercise 04.
  - **DGCL §141(c) — Committees of the board** — the specific mechanic for a board to designate committees and to delegate authority to committees. Referenced in chapter 6 and exercise 06.
  - Non-Delaware equivalents: California Corporations Code §307(b) for the equivalent-of-§141(f); the equivalent state-code section for any other state of incorporation. Note the specific differences from Delaware mechanics per counsel.
- **SEC Regulation S-K Item 407(d)(5) — Audit committee financial expert definition** — [ecfr.gov](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-17/chapter-II/part-229/subpart-229.400/section-229.407) → §229.407(d)(5). The specific criteria a director must meet to qualify as an "audit committee financial expert." Referenced in chapter 6 and exercise 06.
- **SEC — Sarbanes-Oxley Act §301 — Audit committee standards** — the specific SOX requirements for audit committees at public companies. Pre-IPO companies preparing for public status draft charters against the SOX standard. Referenced in chapter 6.
- **NYSE Corporate Governance Standards §303A** — [nyse.com/regulation/listed-company-manual](https://www.nyse.com/regulation/listed-company-manual). Specifically:
  - **§303A.06 — Audit Committee** — the specific NYSE audit-committee requirements.
  - **§303A.07 — Audit Committee Additional Requirements** — the additional specific requirements including charter contents.
  - **§303A.02 — Independence tests** — the specific independence definition for directors at NYSE-listed companies.
  - **§303A.05 — Compensation Committee** — the specific compensation-committee independence and charter requirements.
  - Referenced in chapter 6 and exercise 06.
- **Nasdaq Listing Rules 5605** — [listingcenter.nasdaq.com](https://listingcenter.nasdaq.com/). Specifically:
  - **Rule 5605(c) — Audit Committee** — the Nasdaq-equivalent audit-committee requirements.
  - **Rule 5605(d) — Compensation Committee** — the Nasdaq-equivalent compensation-committee requirements.
  - **Rule 5605(a) — Independent Director definition.**
  - Referenced in chapter 6 and exercise 06.

## Tier 2 — The NVCA legal-document tier

The signed NVCA model documents that govern most US venture-backed private companies. Read alongside the DGCL tier; the NVCA documents encode the specific corporate-formality mechanics for board and committee action at the private-company stage.

- **National Venture Capital Association — Model Legal Documents** — [nvca.org/model-legal-documents/](https://nvca.org/model-legal-documents/). The seven signed model documents:
  - **Model Amended and Restated Certificate of Incorporation.** Encodes the board-composition mechanic (referenced in chapter 6 for committee formation and independent-director requirements), the specific stockholder-vote thresholds for class votes (referenced in exercise 04 for stockholder consents), and the specific written-consent-in-lieu-of-meeting provisions.
  - **Model Bylaws.** Specific thresholds for materiality (contract-value thresholds requiring board approval; the specific bylaws-encoded committee-formation authorities). Referenced in exercise 04.
  - **Model Investors' Rights Agreement (IRA).** Information rights (the specific quarterly-report and annual-audit content the company owes investors); registration rights (relevant at pre-IPO stage per chapter 6); observer rights (relevant to the meeting-design conventions in chapter 3). Referenced in chapters 1, 2, and 3.
  - **Model Voting Agreement.** Board election mechanics — the specific director-election-and-removal process that determines committee-composition dynamics. Referenced in chapter 6.
  - **Model Right of First Refusal / Co-Sale Agreement.** Secondary-transfer approval mechanics — the specific board-approval trigger for a related-party secondary purchase. Referenced in exercise 04.
  - **Model Stock Purchase Agreement (SPA).** Reps and warranties, disclosure schedules, closing conditions — the raise-close mechanics chapter 7's post-close section references.
  - **Model Management Rights Letter (MRL).** VCOC compliance — an investor-specific right to management-level information access; referenced in chapter 7.

## Tier 3 — The practitioner canon — governance and board dynamics

The books and long-form works every CFO responsible for board work should have read.

- **Brad Feld and Mahendra Ramsinghani — *Startup Boards: Getting the Most Out of Your Board of Directors*** — Wiley, currently in the 2nd edition. [startupboards.com](https://startupboards.com/). The single most-cited practitioner reference for startup-board governance. Directly relevant to every chapter of this module. Chapters on board composition, meeting design, board dynamics, executive session, and difficult-conversation navigation are the practitioner backbone of chapters 3, 7, and 8.
- **Bill Gurley — *Above the Crowd*** — [abovethecrowd.com](http://abovethecrowd.com/). Benchmark Capital partner. Specific essays on board dynamics, governance failure modes, and the specific patterns of well-run vs. poorly-run boards. Chapter 3 and chapter 8 reference Gurley on meeting design and honest-signal delivery.
- **Fred Wilson — *AVC*** — [avc.com](https://avc.com/). Long-running blog by the USV managing partner. Wilson has written repeatedly on: monthly investor updates (the pattern chapter 1 traces); board meetings and how to run them (chapters 2 and 3); the CEO-investor relationship (chapter 7); difficult board conversations (chapter 8). "MBA Mondays" archive has multiple governance-adjacent posts.
- **Mark Suster — *Both Sides of the Table*** — [bothsidesofthetable.com](https://bothsidesofthetable.com/). Upfront Ventures partner. Deep archive on board meetings, board-composition dynamics, and specific advice on running productive board meetings. Chapter 3 and chapter 7 draw on Suster's posts.
- **David Sacks — "The Board Meeting"** and adjacent essays — [sacks.substack.com](https://sacks.substack.com/) and archived essays elsewhere. Sacks's specific essays on the board-pack architecture, the strategic-deep-dive discipline, and the failure modes of the walk-the-pack meeting are directly referenced in chapters 2 and 3. His posts on the CEO-CFO partnership are referenced in chapter 7.
- **Ben Horowitz — *The Hard Thing About Hard Things*** — HarperBusiness, 2014. Specific chapters on difficult conversations, on managing a board through a bad quarter, and on the CEO-CFO relationship in crisis. Chapter 8 draws on Horowitz's honest-signal frame.
- **Andy Rachleff, Bill Campbell, and adjacent board-craft canon** — various posts and interviews. The Campbell "coach" archive is a specific reference for the executive-session dynamic in chapter 3.
- **Scott Kupor — *Secrets of Sand Hill Road*** — Portfolio, 2019. a16z managing partner. The inside-the-fund view of how board dynamics look from the investor side. Relevant to chapter 7's investor-side framing.

- **Society for Corporate Governance** — [societyforcorpgov.org](https://www.societyforcorpgov.org/). Governance-professional association; specific resources on committee charters and board-portal implementation.

## Reading order for this module

If you are new to the board-craft and governance canon, this is the order to read:

1. **DGCL §141, §144, and §228.** The statutory reference. Short and well-drafted; read the actual statutes before any practitioner content.
2. **NVCA Model Certificate of Incorporation, Model Bylaws, and Model Investors' Rights Agreement.** The specific corporate-formality mechanics for a venture-backed private company.
3. **Feld & Ramsinghani, *Startup Boards*.** The practitioner canon on board composition, board dynamics, and meeting design. Chapters 3, 7, and 8 of this module draw heavily on it.
4. **Fred Wilson's *AVC* archive on monthly investor updates and board meetings.** The pattern canon for chapters 1 and 3.
5. **David Sacks's essays on board packs and board meetings.** The specific reference for chapter 2's five-section pack architecture and chapter 3's meeting-design discipline.
6. **Mark Suster's *Both Sides of the Table* archive on board dynamics.** Chapter 3 and chapter 8 draw on Suster's specific posts.
7. **SEC Regulation S-K Item 407(d)(5) and NYSE §303A / Nasdaq 5605.** The specific pre-IPO audit-and-compensation-committee references. Read before drafting any committee charter (exercise 06).
8. **Cooley GO's founder-guide articles on board consents, meeting minutes, and committee formation.** The compact practitioner reference for chapters 4 and 6.
9. **NACD reference material.** The governance-institution reference for chapter 6.
10. **Current-vintage Bolster and NACD Director Compensation Report data** for independent-director sourcing and compensation.
11. **Ben Horowitz's *The Hard Thing About Hard Things* chapters on difficult conversations.** The narrative reference for chapter 8's honest-signal frame.
12. **Investor-update template canon (Sequoia network templates, USV-network posts, SaaStr variants).** The template reference for chapter 1 and exercise 01.

Only after that canon should you rely on secondary practitioner content (Substacks, LinkedIn essays, conference talks). Every quantitative claim in a board memo or a decision memo should trace to a tier-1 through tier-4 source, with the vintage named. Every legal citation should be to the current DGCL section, the current SEC rule, or the current stock-exchange listing standard; if the citation cannot be sourced, it is flagged `<!-- needs-research: ... -->` in the chapters, and the same discipline should apply in every consent, charter, and memo the CFO drafts.
