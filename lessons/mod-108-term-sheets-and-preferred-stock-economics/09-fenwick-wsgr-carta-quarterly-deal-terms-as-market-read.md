# Fenwick, Wilson Sonsini, and Carta — Quarterly Deal-Terms Data as the Market Read

## Why this matters

Every clause classification in chapters 2 through 8 — "1x non-participating is market at Series-A," "broad-based weighted-average is market," "2/1/2 board is market," "cumulative dividends are aggressive" — assumes a specific market environment. The classification is not static. When market conditions shift, the specific-term incidence shifts, and what was tier-1 market in a founder-favourable market becomes tier-2 negotiated in a stressed market. A CFO who negotiates against a stale classification (or worse, against a classification held in someone's head from an unrelated market period) opens the negotiation from an indefensible position.

The disciplined check is the quarterly deal-terms data. Three sources dominate as term-sheet-terms references:

- **Fenwick & West Silicon Valley Venture Survey** — the longest-running quarterly report on Silicon Valley venture term-sheet feature frequencies. Consistent methodology back to 2002.
- **Wilson Sonsini Entrepreneurs Report** — quarterly, national coverage, particularly strong on seed and early-stage detail and on bridge-round terms.
- **Carta State of Private Markets and Carta liquidation-preference data** — Carta's proprietary cap-table data set covering a large number of Carta-managed cap tables, with the most granular term-frequency cuts available publicly.

[Mod-106 chapter 6](../mod-106-startup-valuation-frameworks/06-fenwick-wsgr-pitchbook-nvca-deal-terms.md) covered these three sources for *valuation* — median pre-moneys by stage, deal count, capital deployed. This chapter re-uses the same sources for *term-sheet features* — participating-preferred incidence, anti-dilution formula frequencies, board-composition patterns, redemption and dividend frequencies, drag-along and MFN incidence — and covers the reading discipline the CFO uses to anchor every clause classification to the current-quarter position.

## What Fenwick publishes on term-sheet features

Fenwick's quarterly *Silicon Valley Venture Survey* has a dedicated section on the term-sheet feature frequencies. The specific data cuts include:

**Liquidation preference features:**

- **Percentage of financings with senior-vs.-pari-passu liquidation preferences.** Series-A / B / C / D+ breakdowns.
- **Percentage of financings with 1x, 1x-2x, 2x-3x, and greater-than-3x preference multiples.**
- **Percentage of financings with non-participating, participating (capped), and participating (uncapped) preferred.**
- **Percentage of financings with pay-to-play provisions.**

**Anti-dilution features:**

- **Percentage of financings with broad-based weighted-average, narrow-based weighted-average, or full ratchet.** Trend over multiple quarters.

**Dividend features:**

- **Percentage of financings with dividend rate 0%, non-cumulative, cumulative.**
- **Percentage of financings with dividends that convert to common at conversion of the preferred vs. paid at liquidation.**

**Redemption and other:**

- **Percentage of financings with redemption rights.** Trigger period, price mechanic.
- **Percentage of financings with cumulative dividends.**
- **Percentage of financings with a specific-series (rather than combined) class vote on protective provisions.**

The strength of Fenwick as a term-sheet-features source:

- **Consistent methodology across quarters.** The time series is directly comparable back to 2002, which allows meaningful long-run trend analysis.
- **Explicit percentages, not just descriptive commentary.** A CFO reading Fenwick can cite specific quarterly percentages.
- **Barometer-style summary.** The Fenwick & West Barometer measures round-over-round price change, which correlates with term-sheet feature trends (down-round-heavy quarters show elevated participating-preferred incidence).

The weakness:

- **Silicon Valley scope only.** For companies outside California, Fenwick is a partial data point.
- **Sample bias.** Fenwick reports on financings where the firm has visibility. Not a random sample of the market.
- **Coverage of very early stages is thin.** Series-A onward is the strong zone; pre-seed and seed are less covered.

## What Wilson Sonsini publishes on term-sheet features

The Wilson Sonsini Entrepreneurs Report covers similar data cuts to Fenwick but with materially broader scope:

- **National geographic scope** rather than Silicon Valley only.
- **Better seed and Series-A coverage** by financing count.
- **Bridge-round data** as a distinct category with its own term-sheet feature frequencies.
- **Convertible-instrument data** (SAFE cap distributions, note discount ranges) — relevant to the SAFE-conversion terms of the priced round in this module.
- **Life-sciences-vs.-tech breakdown** for some data points, useful for CFOs at life-sciences-adjacent companies.

Specific term-sheet feature cuts published by Wilson Sonsini:

- **Median and mean pre-money valuations by round type**, cross-tabulated with **percentage of financings that were up-rounds, flat rounds, or down-rounds.**
- **Percentage of financings with participating preferred, broken out by series.**
- **Percentage of financings with senior-vs.-pari-passu preferences.**
- **Anti-dilution formula frequencies.**
- **Board-composition patterns** in some vintages — number of seats, founder-vs.-investor-vs.-independent breakdowns.
- **Drag-along mechanics and price floors.**
- **Pro-rata rights (frequency, Major Investor thresholds).**
- **Redemption and cumulative-dividend frequencies.**
- **Bridge-round-specific terms** — extension SAFE frequencies, insider-led bridge note frequencies, preference stacking on bridges.

Wilson Sonsini's advantages over Fenwick for this module's purposes: broader geographic coverage, better bridge-round data (relevant to chapter 8 and to [mod-109](../mod-109-runway-management-and-bridge-financing/)), and better seed detail. Wilson Sonsini's disadvantages: methodology has changed somewhat across vintages (creating small breaks in the time series that Fenwick doesn't have), and the specific data cuts published vary quarter-to-quarter (so the CFO can't always find the same metric in every quarterly report).

## What Carta publishes on term-sheet features

Carta's data set is different in nature from Fenwick and Wilson Sonsini. Where the two law firms report on financings where they have visibility (a sample), Carta reports on the full population of cap tables managed on the Carta platform (which represents a large and growing share of US venture-backed companies, particularly at earlier stages). Carta's specific advantages:

- **Sample size.** Vastly larger than either law-firm sample.
- **Granular term breakdowns.** Because Carta parses actual cap tables (not just term-sheet summaries), it can extract specific details like "median preference multiple by series and by round vintage" or "distribution of pool sizes by financing size."
- **Liquidation-preference-specific data.** Carta publishes specific pieces on liquidation-preference data — participating vs. non-participating incidence, senior stack incidence, preference stacking by stage.
- **Bridge-round and down-round frequency by quarter.** Because the cap tables reflect actual completed financings, Carta can compute down-round-frequency directly.
- **Down-round terms.** Carta publishes on specific down-round patterns (pay-to-play frequency, wash-out frequency, preference-stack changes at down rounds).
- **ESOP top-up sizing at each round.**
- **Founder-dilution-through-Series-C-and-later cuts.**

Carta's disadvantages:

- **Platform bias.** Carta's dataset is companies on Carta. This skews toward Silicon Valley / West Coast, toward tech, and toward companies that adopted Carta at seed (as opposed to migrated on later). The bias is smaller than it was a few years ago as Carta's adoption has grown, but it exists.
- **Specific-report cadence.** Carta publishes the *State of Private Markets* on a defined quarterly cadence, but individual data cuts on liquidation preference, dividends, and other specific term-sheet features appear in one-off Carta blog posts and reports rather than in a consistent quarterly cadence. The CFO tracking a specific term over time may find gaps.

## The interpretive discipline — reading three sources together

For any specific clause classification in chapters 2-8, the CFO's disciplined check is:

1. **Look up the current-quarter incidence** of the clause across all three sources (Fenwick, Wilson Sonsini, Carta) where the specific-clause data is available.
2. **Read the trailing 4-quarter trend.** Is the specific-clause incidence rising, flat, or falling?
3. **Cross-check against the specific stage.** Series-A specifically? Series-B? Growth?
4. **Anchor the classification** — "market" means the most-common structure at the target stage in the current quarter, not the most-common structure at a different stage or in a different quarter.
5. **Note trend direction.** If the specific-clause incidence is rising (say, participating preferred at Series-A is rising from 15% to 20% over 4 quarters), the classification shift is in progress — a clause that was "aggressive" is drifting toward "negotiated but market." Update the CFO's tier assignment accordingly.

**Worked example.** Suppose the CFO is negotiating a Series-A term sheet with 1x participating preferred (capped at 3x). The chapter 2 baseline classification is "aggressive but with mitigations." The current-quarter data check:

- Fenwick current-quarter: 18% of Series-A financings have participating preferred. Up from 14% four quarters ago.
- Wilson Sonsini current-quarter: 15% of Series-A financings have participating preferred. Also up.
- Carta current-quarter: 17% of Series-A financings on Carta platform have participating preferred. Also up.

Reading: participating preferred at Series-A is running at ~15-18% incidence and drifting upward. This is elevated relative to the historical low-single-digits range but still a clear minority. The CFO's classification: participating preferred is tier-2 negotiated (not tier-1 market, not tier-3 aggressive) in the current environment, with the trend suggesting continued drift. The negotiation posture: push for non-participating; if the lead insists, cap the participation as low as possible and price the concession into the pre-money (i.e., accept participating at a higher pre-money).

**A contrasting scenario.** Fenwick current-quarter: 6% of Series-A financings have participating preferred. Flat over trailing 4 quarters. Wilson Sonsini and Carta similar. Reading: participating preferred is genuinely tier-3 aggressive in the current environment. Push hard against it; the counter is well-supported by the market data.

## What Carta specifically publishes on preference structures

Carta's *State of Private Markets* and Carta's liquidation-preference-specific research posts historically cover:

- **Percentage of Carta-managed rounds with 1x preference multiple** (typically >95% at Series-A).
- **Percentage with participating vs. non-participating preferred** by stage.
- **Percentage of Series-B and later rounds with senior stack vs. pari passu.**
- **Percentage of rounds with pay-to-play provisions.**
- **Percentage of rounds with redemption rights.**
- **Down-round frequency by quarter.**
- **Bridge-round frequency by quarter.**
- **ESOP size changes across rounds.**
- **Preference-stack aggregate as a percentage of post-money** — a useful metric for understanding how much of the company's value is "sitting under" the common in the preference stack.

The last item is particularly useful for exit-scenario planning: at a given company, aggregate preference stack / post-money valuation = X%. If the exit lands at less than X% of post-money, the common gets zero. If the exit lands at 2X% of post-money, the common gets the middle-band waterfall. Carta's cuts on this metric across the venture-backed universe give the CFO a benchmark for interpretation.

## The market-conditions read and the negotiation stance

The current-quarter market position shifts the CFO's negotiation stance in specific ways:

**Founder-favourable market (up-round dominant, participating preferred low, no dividend accrual):**

- Push harder against any deviation from 1x non-participating.
- Refuse redemption, cumulative dividends, MFN outright.
- Insist on 2 founder + 1 investor + 2 independent board.
- Assume the negotiation environment supports founder-favourable positions.

**Stressed market (down-round percentage elevated, participating preferred rising, bridge round frequency elevated):**

- Prepare for lead-to-push-back on non-participating; be ready with capped-participation compromise language.
- Prepare for cumulative-dividend request; be ready with rate-and-mechanic counter.
- Prepare for redemption request; be ready with deferred-trigger counter.
- Prepare for senior-stack request at Series-B; be ready with pari-passu counter.
- Consider giving on non-headline clauses in exchange for headline (pre-money, preference structure).

**Sector-specific market conditions.** Some sectors run against the general venture-market backdrop. Bio, deeptech, and climate frequently have different term-sheet feature frequencies than software. The CFO working in a specific sector should read sector-specific data where available (Wilson Sonsini publishes life-sciences-specific cuts; Carta and PitchBook publish sector reports) rather than relying only on aggregate market data.

## The term-sheet-context memo

The output of reading the three sources for term-sheet features is a **term-sheet-context memo** that anchors the negotiation posture for a specific fundraise. Suggested structure:

**Section 1 — Macro market read (from mod-106 chapter 6).**

- Overall venture activity (deals, capital deployed) direction.
- Up-round vs. down-round frequencies at the target stage.
- Read: market is founder-favourable / balanced / stressed.

**Section 2 — Stage-specific term-sheet features.**

- **Preference structure.** Percentage 1x non-participating / participating (with cap distribution) / other, at target stage, current quarter and trailing.
- **Anti-dilution formula.** Percentage broad-based / narrow-based / full ratchet, at target stage.
- **Pro-rata rights.** Percentage of rounds with pro-rata for Major Investors; percentage with super-pro-rata; percentage with lead-pro-rata reserved allocations.
- **Protective provisions.** Any data on list length or specific-item incidence. Fenwick and Wilson Sonsini publish item-by-item incidence in some vintages.
- **Board composition.** Any data on typical board size and composition breakdown at target stage.
- **Drag-along, tag-along, redemption, dividend, MFN.** Frequency and specific-parameter data where published.

**Section 3 — Trend direction on each feature.**

- Which features are drifting more investor-favourable?
- Which features are drifting more founder-favourable?
- Any features with a clear turning-point signal (e.g., participating preferred just crossed a threshold)?

**Section 4 — Application to the specific term sheet.**

- For each clause in the received term sheet, the classification against the current-quarter data.
- The specific negotiation posture (accept / negotiate at market / push against / refuse).
- The bundling and prioritisation.

**Section 5 — Fallback positions.**

- If the primary negotiation ask on a clause fails, what is the next-best structure the CFO would accept? Draft the language in advance.
- Which clauses are must-haves and which are worth trading?

The memo ships to the CEO before the negotiation opens and to counsel as an input to the red-line. It's refreshed as the negotiation progresses if the market data updates within the negotiation window (uncommon, but the CFO who has read the memo before opening does not have to re-read the reports during the negotiation).

## The "seasonal" or "cyclical" pattern in the data

Term-sheet feature frequencies move on a cyclical pattern:

- **In founder-favourable markets** (excess capital, competitive funding environment, high up-round percentage), term-sheet features drift founder-favourable: participating-preferred incidence falls, board structures move toward 2/1/2, protective-provisions lists shorten, dividend accrual falls to zero-non-cumulative, redemption disappears.
- **In investor-favourable markets** (capital scarce, competitive company environment, high down-round percentage), the drift reverses: participating-preferred incidence rises, board structures shift investor-favourable, protective-provisions lists lengthen, cumulative dividends appear, redemption appears.
- **The lag from macro conditions to term-sheet features is real.** Macro deal-count and capital-deployed data lead term-sheet-feature data by 1-2 quarters typically. If the macro market has just turned stressed, term-sheet features may still reflect the prior founder-favourable environment for another quarter or two before they catch up.

The CFO's read should account for this lag. In a market that has just turned stressed, some term sheets will still be drafted against the prior founder-favourable template; the CFO can either negotiate them at the outgoing standard (opportunistic) or expect the lead to push toward the incoming standard (defensive).

## Common founder traps

- **Reading a single source.** Fenwick's Silicon Valley scope, Wilson Sonsini's national scope, and Carta's platform-based cap-table data each have biases. Triangulate.
- **Reading a single quarter without the trend.** Term-sheet feature frequencies shift; a snapshot without direction is incomplete.
- **Reading aggregate data without stage specificity.** Series-A and Series-D preferences move differently; aggregate medians don't help a specific fundraise.
- **Ignoring the sector.** Software, bio, deeptech, climate have different feature distributions.
- **Cited data being stale.** A CFO citing "the Fenwick report shows participating preferred at X%" without noting the report date opens up a challenge from the lead ("that report is from six quarters ago").
- **Confusing valuation data with term-sheet feature data.** The pre-money median tells you what a specific fundraise should target for the *price*; the participating-preferred incidence tells you what the *structure* should be. Both matter; they're not the same.
- **Not accounting for the lag from macro to term-sheet features.** If the macro just turned, the term-sheet data may lag; adjust the negotiation posture forward-looking rather than backward-looking.
- **Ignoring the data because "our fund uses their template regardless of market conditions."** Even funds with template term sheets adjust the specific parameter values (multiples, thresholds, list lengths) based on market conditions. The data still matters.

## What good looks like

A CFO who has this material installed:

- Reads the current-quarter Fenwick, Wilson Sonsini, and Carta reports before opening any term-sheet negotiation.
- Reads the trailing 4-quarter trend for each material clause.
- Cross-checks the specific-stage data (Series-A, Series-B, growth) against the current classification.
- Produces the term-sheet-context memo with the specific-clause classification and the negotiation posture.
- Cites specific data points in the negotiation ("Fenwick's most recent report shows participating preferred at 18% of Series-A financings, up from 14% four quarters ago").
- Adjusts the negotiation posture for the current market conditions rather than defaulting to a stale classification.
- Refreshes the market-conditions read at each successive term-sheet negotiation as the underlying data updates.

## Summary

- Term-sheet-clause classifications ("market-standard vs. aggressive vs. unusual") are not static. They drift with market conditions, and the CFO's job is to read the current-quarter position before opening the negotiation.
- Three quarterly deal-terms data sources dominate: Fenwick Silicon Valley Venture Survey (long-run consistent methodology, Silicon Valley scope), Wilson Sonsini Entrepreneurs Report (national scope, seed/bridge data), and Carta State of Private Markets and Carta liquidation-preference research (platform-based cap-table data, granular cuts).
- Each has specific strengths and specific biases. Triangulation across the three produces the defensible term-sheet-context read.
- The CFO's output is a term-sheet-context memo covering macro market read, stage-specific term-sheet feature frequencies, trend direction, application to the specific term sheet, and fallback positions.
- Term-sheet feature frequencies are cyclical: founder-favourable markets drift term-sheet features founder-favourable; stressed markets reverse the drift. Lag from macro conditions to term-sheet features is real and should be accounted for in the negotiation posture.
- Common failure modes: single-source reading, single-quarter without trend, aggregate-not-stage-specific, ignoring sector, stale citation, confusing valuation data with term-sheet-feature data.

This chapter closes the module. Every clause in chapters 2-8 has a classification; every classification is anchored to the current-quarter data. Chapter 1's four-tier framework, applied against chapter 9's quarterly data read, produces the defensible term-sheet-negotiation posture that structures the exercises that follow.
