# Exercise 08 — Term-Sheet Negotiation Role-Play

**Estimated time:** ~8 hours (across 3-4 sessions)
**Prerequisites:** All nine chapters and exercises 01-07. This is the capstone exercise; it exercises every mechanic in the module against a single running scenario.

## Problem statement

Run an end-to-end Series-A term-sheet negotiation, from received offer through executed term sheet, as a two-sided role-play. Produce every artefact a real negotiation produces: the received term sheet, the CFO-side prioritisation memo, the counter-proposal, three rounds of back-and-forth with the lead, the executed term sheet, and the closing memo to the board. The exercise is designed to be run either (a) solo with the founder alternating between founder-side and investor-side roles, using the earlier exercises' outputs as the reference, or (b) in a pair with a partner playing the investor side.

The capstone integrates the module: chapter 1's four-tier classification (exercise 01), chapter 2-3's liquidation-preference and waterfall math (exercise 02), chapter 4's anti-dilution mechanics (exercise 03), chapter 5's pro-rata (exercise 04), chapter 6's protective provisions (exercise 05), chapter 7's board composition (exercise 06), chapter 9's market-conditions read (exercise 07), and chapter 8's quiet clauses. The point is not any single mechanic — it is the *integration*: negotiating twenty clauses simultaneously against a moving counterparty over three-to-four rounds, holding the priorities, spending negotiation capital where it matters, and closing.

## Scenario — the round in front of you

Use a consistent company across all rounds of the role-play. Recommended: use the Series-A candidate from exercise 01 or the mod-107 company-B profile. Alternatively, take a real company (anonymised) that has recently raised Series A.

**Company assumptions.**

- Company at $2.4M ARR, growing 8% MoM, 24 employees. B2B SaaS in a defined vertical.
- Cap table: founders 55%, employees 12% (options granted + issued), seed preferred 18%, SAFE stack ~7%, unissued pool ~8%.
- Raising Series A: target $15M new money at $50M pre / $65M post (target dilution ~23%, target ownership for the incoming lead ~20-22%). Pool top-up to 12% post-close, placed pre-money.
- Two term sheets in hand — a lead-A term sheet (this is the primary; the other is a competitive backup used in the role-play).

**Round timing.** Each negotiation round is a written exchange plus a summary-of-conversation note. Do not try to compress into a single day; realistic Series-A negotiations run 10-30 days from received term sheet to executed term sheet. The role-play should preserve that pace with a "day X" log.

## Requirements

Produce the following artefacts in a submission directory, dated by "day X of the negotiation."

### Setup

1. **Company brief** (`day-00-company-brief.md`). One page. Company context, current cap table, milestone bar for the next round, current-quarter market environment (from exercise 07), the CEO's stated priorities across control (board), economics (preference and pre-money), and speed (close timing).

### Round 0 — the received offer

2. **Received term sheet — Lead A** (`day-01-lead-a-term-sheet.md`). A full Series-A term sheet from Lead A. Use exercise 01's drafting method: NVCA-model term sheet as base, edited to include a plausible mix of tier-1 through tier-4 clauses. This is the negotiation-primary offer.

3. **Received term sheet — Lead B** (`day-02-lead-b-term-sheet.md`). A meaningfully-different competing term sheet from Lead B. Different pre-money, different preference structure, different board composition. This is the backup / leverage.

4. **CFO prioritisation memo** (`day-03-cfo-prioritisation-memo.md`, 2 pages). Structure per exercise 01:
   - Bottom-line summary of both term sheets.
   - The clause-by-clause classification for each (compact — reference exercise 01's table method).
   - The three-to-five must-win asks against Lead A.
   - The concessions.
   - The current-quarter market-conditions anchor (per exercise 07).
   - The compare-and-choose recommendation between Lead A and Lead B.

### Round 1 — the CFO's counter

5. **Round-1 counter-proposal** (`day-05-round1-counter.md`). A red-line of Lead A's term sheet showing the specific changes the CFO is asking for on the three-to-five P1 items and any P2 items worth surfacing. Do not red-line every clause. Include:
   - The specific new language for each red-line.
   - A one-paragraph cover note explaining the counter's shape.
   - Explicit acceptance of items that are being conceded (do not silently drop them from the red-line without a signal).

6. **Round-1 conversation log** (`day-06-round1-conversation-log.md`). A written summary of the "phone call" / "email exchange" with Lead A's partner and counsel in which the counter is presented. Bullet-formatted. Include the specific talking points, the anticipated push-back, and the specific data points cited from exercise 07's market-conditions read.

### Round 2 — the lead's response

7. **Round-2 response from Lead A** (`day-09-round2-lead-response.md`). A red-line back from Lead A. Play the lead-side authentically: the lead will concede on some items, hold on others, and offer trades on the rest. Structure the response as:
   - Accepts (2-3 items where the CFO's counter is accepted).
   - Rejects (1-2 items where Lead A holds firm; usually the P1 items where they have the most at stake).
   - Splits the difference (2-3 items where a middle position is offered).
   - New asks (0-1 items where the lead surfaces something not in the original term sheet — e.g., a specific ROFR / co-sale drafting request; a specific IRA information-rights parameter).

### Round 3 — the final push

8. **Round-3 counter with trades** (`day-12-round3-counter.md`). The CFO's response to round 2. Trades: concede on one P2 to hold a P1; accept a split-the-difference on one item to move a hardline. Include:
   - The specific trade proposals.
   - The Lead-B leverage (if any) — cite the alternative term sheet without threatening ("we would accept X on the pre-money if Y on the preference, which is closer to what we see in [alternative competing offer]").
   - The specific closing gesture — a signal that this is the CFO's final counter and the CEO is ready to execute if the lead accepts.

9. **Round-3 conversation log** (`day-13-round3-conversation-log.md`). Written summary of the "call" in which the final counter is presented and the executed term is agreed.

### Executed term sheet and close

10. **Executed term sheet** (`day-15-executed-term-sheet.md`). The final agreed term sheet — the merged, signed version. Every clause reconciled between the original Lead A, the CFO's asks, and the negotiation trades.

11. **Executed-term-sheet reconciliation** (`day-15-reconciliation.md`, 1 page). A compact clause-by-clause table:
    - Original Lead A position.
    - CFO's counter position (round 1).
    - Final agreed position.
    - The negotiation trade or rationale that produced the final.

12. **Closing memo to the board** (`day-16-board-closing-memo.md`, 2 pages). Structure:
    - Bottom-line summary of the executed term sheet: pre-money, raise, ownership breakdown, dilution, board composition, protective-provisions posture, preference structure.
    - The three specific negotiation wins from the CFO's P1 asks.
    - The three concessions.
    - The comparison against the offered Lead A original.
    - The two-year forward view: how the executed term sheet positions the company for Series-B.
    - The next-30-days close plan: definitive documents drafting owners, disclosure schedules, closing conditions, expected closing date.

## Starter guidance

- **Play the lead's side seriously.** The exercise fails if the CFO caves at every round-2 push-back or if the lead concedes every P1 ask. Realistic lead-side behaviour: partial concessions, hold-firms on the top-1-2 economic items, and specific counter-asks the CFO did not anticipate. If running solo, discipline yourself to "play the lead" as if you were the lead partner defending the term sheet to the fund's investment committee.
- **Use exercise 07's market-conditions data as the anchor.** Every negotiation talking point should trace to a specific market-data citation or an internal (company-specific) rationale. "Because I feel strongly" is not a negotiation position; "Because current Fenwick data shows 1x non-participating at 85% of Series-A financings and the concession costs us $4M at the median exit" is.
- **Do not red-line more than 5-6 clauses in round 1.** Chapter 1's discipline: pick the fights that matter. If the round-1 counter has 15 red-lines, the exercise has not compressed the priority read.
- **Show the negotiation capital budget.** Track — either in the conversation logs or in a side note — which asks are P1 (spend capital), P2 (surface, negotiate softly), P3 (concede early to signal reasonableness). The pattern of "concede on P3 in round 1, hold on P1 through round 3" is the specific behaviour a good CFO exhibits.
- **The Lead-B leverage is real but should be used carefully.** Never overplay it; never explicitly threaten. The load-bearing move is to make the alternative visible without making it a threat. Overplayed Lead-B leverage causes the primary lead to walk.
- **Reconcile the executed term sheet against both original term sheets.** The executed term sheet is not just "Lead A modified"; it is "the best possible term sheet given the alternatives." The reconciliation memo should trace which clauses were negotiated vs. which alternatives.
- **The board-facing closing memo is the artefact the CFO is judged on.** A CEO or board reading it should understand exactly what was negotiated, what was traded, and what the go-forward implications are for Series-B. Two pages.

## Acceptance criteria

- **All twelve artefacts are produced** with the specified naming and dating.
- **The two received term sheets are meaningfully different.** Not cosmetic differences; substantive differences in preference structure, board composition, or economics.
- **The prioritisation memo names three-to-five P1 asks** with market-data anchors.
- **The round-1 counter red-lines only the P1 and select-P2 items.** Fewer than five red-lines suggests under-classification; more than eight suggests the priority discipline broke.
- **The round-2 lead response is realistic** — concedes some, holds on others, surfaces at least one new ask. The lead is not a punching bag.
- **The round-3 counter includes at least two explicit trades.** Concede A to hold B. Signal willingness to close if the trade is accepted.
- **The executed term sheet is internally consistent.** Numbers reconcile (pre-money, post-money, share count, ownership breakdown). No orphan clauses that contradict each other.
- **The reconciliation table has every clause traced** from Lead A original → CFO counter → executed. No unexplained changes.
- **The board closing memo names three wins and three concessions** and the two-year forward view.
- **The pace of the negotiation is realistic.** Dated logs across 15-16 days; not compressed into a single day. Not stretched across 60 days.
- **The market-conditions data cited traces to specific sources** — every quantitative claim cited to Fenwick / WSGR / Carta with a specific vintage, or flagged `<!-- needs-research -->`.

## Extensions (optional)

- **Add a "no-shop violation" wrinkle.** After round 2, Lead A discovers that the CEO had a subsequent conversation with a third party (Lead C, previously undisclosed) which arguably violates the no-shop. Play through the handling — the CFO's diplomatic response, the counsel-facing note, the re-anchoring of Lead A's confidence.
- **Add a due-diligence surprise.** During the round-2-to-round-3 window, Lead A's diligence uncovers a rep concern (a customer contract that arguably doesn't reconcile with the ARR representation, or a co-founder-departure situation the CEO hadn't disclosed). Play through the disclosure, the impact on the term sheet (specific reps, escrow, indemnification), and the specific counter.
- **Play the "walk away" scenario.** Round 3, the lead refuses to move on the P1 item. Play through the walk-away decision — the CFO's assessment of whether Lead B is a real alternative, the CEO's decision, the walk-away letter, and the Lead-B negotiation from scratch.
- **Add a second co-lead.** During round 2, Lead A brings in a co-lead (Lead A2) who will take 30% of the round. The co-lead has slightly different terms preferences — specifically, wants observer rights and a specific pro-rata parameter. Play through the tri-party negotiation.
- **Extend to definitive documents.** After executed term sheet, spend two more days on the definitive-document drafting review: SPA disclosure schedules, IRA information-rights parameters, Voting Agreement independent-director nomination language, the restated Certificate of Incorporation reflecting the executed preference and anti-dilution terms. Draft or red-line one specific definitive document.
- **Preview mod-109 by adding a Series-B fast-forward.** 18 months post-close, model the same executed term sheet's carry-through to a Series-B negotiation. Which clauses (protective provisions, pro-rata, board seats) constrain the Series-B negotiation? Which were the far-sighted wins from the Series-A negotiation?
