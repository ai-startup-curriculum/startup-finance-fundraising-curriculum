# Resources — mod-108 Term Sheets & Preferred-Stock Economics

This is the reference stack that sits behind every chapter and exercise in this module. Sources are grouped by tier of authority. The NVCA Model Legal Documents are the load-bearing tier-1 reference; every clause classification in chapters 1-8 assumes the current NVCA vintage. The Fenwick / WSGR / Carta quarterly deal-terms reports are the current-market anchor; every quantitative "market-standard" claim in the module should be re-verified against the most recent published quarter before it goes into a live term-sheet negotiation.

Because term-sheet-feature incidence — participating-preferred frequency, anti-dilution formula distribution, board-composition patterns, redemption and dividend frequencies — moves materially quarter-to-quarter and vintage-to-vintage, always cite the specific report vintage when quoting a number in a memo or a counter-proposal. The `<!-- needs-research -->` markers in the chapters flag places where the current-vintage data should be re-verified against the most recent publication of the source before being cited to counsel or the CEO.

## Tier 1 — The industry reference stack

The load-bearing primary sources. Read these first.

- **National Venture Capital Association — Model Legal Documents** — [nvca.org/model-legal-documents/](https://nvca.org/model-legal-documents/). The seven signed model documents that every US Series-A financing is drafted against:
  - **Model Term Sheet** — the 2-4 page non-binding summary. Chapter 1's four-tier classification is calibrated against the parameters in this document.
  - **Model Stock Purchase Agreement (SPA)** — the definitive purchase-and-sale document, including reps and warranties, closing conditions, and indemnification. Chapter 1.
  - **Model Investors' Rights Agreement (IRA)** — registration rights, information rights, pro-rata / preemptive rights. Chapter 5 anchors the pro-rata mechanics here; chapters 1 and 8 reference the information-rights and registration-rights sections.
  - **Model Right of First Refusal / Co-Sale Agreement (ROFR / Co-Sale)** — founder secondary transfer mechanics. Chapter 8.
  - **Model Voting Agreement** — board election and drag-along. Chapters 7 and 8 anchor their drafting notes here.
  - **Model Amended and Restated Certificate of Incorporation** — the corporate charter that encodes the preferred class's rights (dividend, liquidation preference, voting, conversion, anti-dilution, redemption, protective provisions). Chapters 2, 3, 4, 6, and 8 all reference specific sections of the Model Certificate.
  - **Model Management Rights Letter (MRL)** — VCOC compliance under ERISA. Chapter 1.
- **NVCA — Venture Monitor / Yearbook and industry advocacy content** — [nvca.org](https://nvca.org/). The trade association's data releases and policy content. Not directly cited in the module for term-sheet-feature incidence (Fenwick / WSGR / Carta are the term-feature references), but useful contextual reading for the CFO on the state of the venture industry.

## Tier 2 — The practitioner canon

The books and long-form works every fundraising-finance lead should have read.

- **Brad Feld and Jason Mendelson — *Venture Deals: Be Smarter Than Your Lawyer and Venture Capitalist*** — Wiley, currently in the 5th or later edition. [venturedeals.com](https://www.venturedeals.com/). The single most-cited practitioner reference for venture-deal mechanics. Chapters on term-sheet economics, control terms, valuation, dilution, and negotiation are directly relevant. Chapters 1, 2, 4, 5, 6, and 7 of this module all draw on Feld & Mendelson.
- **Scott Kupor — *Secrets of Sand Hill Road: Venture Capital and How to Get It*** — Portfolio, 2019. Written by a16z's managing partner. Inside-the-fund view of how the preference stack, board composition, and protective provisions look from the investor side. Chapters 2 and 3's preference-stack sections and chapter 7's board dynamics benefit from Kupor's investor-side framing.
- **Mark Suster — *Both Sides of the Table*** — [bothsidesofthetable.com](https://bothsidesofthetable.com/). Long-running blog by the Upfront Ventures partner. Deep archive on term-sheet mechanics, preference structures, board composition, and negotiation posture. Multiple posts specifically on liquidation-preference structure and board-composition dynamics that chapters 2, 3, and 7 draw on.
- **Fred Wilson — *AVC*** — [avc.com](https://avc.com/). Long-running blog by the USV managing partner. Multiple essays on term-sheet economics and market-standard drift. Chapter 9's market-conditions read benefits from Wilson's periodic market-condition essays.
- **Bill Gurley — *Above the Crowd*** — [abovethecrowd.com](http://abovethecrowd.com/). Benchmark Capital partner. Specific essays on unicorn valuations, preference stacking in growth-stage rounds ("*On the Road to Recap*" is a canonical reference on preference-stack pathology at growth stages), and dirty-term-sheet dynamics.
- **Alex Rampell — *a16z content on late-stage preference stacking*** — [a16z.com](https://a16z.com/). Late-stage-specific content on how the preference stack evolves at Series-C and beyond.

## Tier 3 — Legal-and-market reference publications

The quarterly and periodic law-firm publications that anchor the "market-standard" drift.

- **Fenwick & West — Silicon Valley Venture Survey / Fenwick VC Barometer** — [fenwick.com](https://www.fenwick.com/) → Insights. The longest-running quarterly deal-terms report. Consistent methodology back to 2002. Covers preference multiples and participation, seniority stacking, anti-dilution formulae, protective-provisions patterns, redemption, dividend rates, board-composition indicators, and pre-money-per-share direction (up / flat / down rounds). Chapter 9 is anchored on Fenwick as the primary source.
- **Wilson Sonsini Goodrich & Rosati — WSGR Entrepreneurs Report** — [wsgr.com](https://www.wsgr.com/) → Insights. Quarterly / periodic national-scope report on deal terms. Better seed and Series-A coverage than Fenwick; better bridge-round data (which cross-references [mod-109](../mod-109-runway-management-and-bridge-financing/) directly); breaks out life-sciences vs. tech. Chapter 9 uses WSGR as the second primary source.
- **Cooley GO / Cooley Insights** — [cooleygo.com](https://www.cooleygo.com/) and [cooley.com/news/insights](https://www.cooley.com/news/insights). Cooley's free founder-facing legal content covers term-sheet mechanics, preference structures, cap-table implications, and periodic "Cooley Venture Financing Report" data on term-sheet features. Referenced across chapters 1, 2, 4, 6, and 8. Cooley's "founder guide" articles are a good compact reference for many specific clauses.
- **Gunderson Dettmer — Emerging Companies / Venture Capital content** — [gunder.com](https://www.gunder.com/) → Insights. Gunderson's periodic content on term-sheet trends, particularly relevant for Series-A and Series-B feature patterns.
- **Orrick — Total Access / Orrick Insights** — [orrick.com](https://www.orrick.com/) → Insights. Orrick's founder-facing content on formation, financing, and term-sheet mechanics.
- **Latham & Watkins — Emerging Companies content** — [lw.com](https://www.lw.com/en/insights) — periodic term-sheet-features and market-conditions content.
- **Goodwin — Technology Group / Venture Capital publications** — [goodwinlaw.com](https://www.goodwinlaw.com/) → Insights.
- **Perkins Coie — Emerging Companies / Venture Capital content** — [perkinscoie.com](https://perkinscoie.com/) → Insights. Perkins Coie's periodic venture-terms content, particularly on the West Coast and Pacific Northwest financings.

## Reading order for this module

If you are new to the term-sheet-mechanics canon, this is the order to read:

1. **NVCA Model Term Sheet and Model Certificate of Incorporation.** The load-bearing reference. Read them once cover-to-cover before starting chapter 2.
2. **Feld & Mendelson, *Venture Deals*.** Chapters on term-sheet economics (liquidation preference, anti-dilution, pro-rata), control terms (protective provisions, board), and negotiation. The practitioner canon.
3. **Kupor, *Secrets of Sand Hill Road*.** The investor-side view of preference stacking, board dynamics, and protective provisions. Complements Feld & Mendelson.
4. **Suster's *Both Sides of the Table*** archive on term-sheet mechanics and preference structures. Specific essays on 1x participating vs. 1x non-participating, protective-provisions negotiation, and board composition.
5. **Wilson's *AVC*** essays on stage-specific term-sheet dynamics and market drift.
6. **Cooley GO** on specific clauses: liquidation preferences, anti-dilution formulae, protective provisions, board composition. Cooley's founder-guide articles are compact and useful.
7. **Current-vintage Fenwick, WSGR, and Carta** deal-terms reports. Read the most recent quarter for each; skim two or three prior quarters for trend context.
8. **NVCA Model Investors' Rights Agreement, Voting Agreement, and ROFR / Co-Sale Agreement.** Read after the Model Certificate; the interdependencies become clear.
9. **NVCA Model Stock Purchase Agreement and Management Rights Letter.** The definitive-document reading, useful before drafting the disclosure schedules.
10. **Sector-specific benchmark sources** if the specific negotiation is in a stage-specific sector (SaaS-heavy, marketplace-heavy, hardware, life-sciences).

Only after that canon should you rely on secondary practitioner content (Substacks, LinkedIn essays, conference talks). Every quantitative claim in a term-sheet memo or a counter-proposal should trace to a tier-1 through tier-4 source, with the vintage named. Assertions that cannot be sourced are flagged `<!-- needs-research: ... -->` in the chapters; the same discipline should apply in every counter-memo the CFO writes.
