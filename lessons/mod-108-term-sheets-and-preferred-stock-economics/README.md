**Track:** [Startup Finance & Fundraising](../../CURRICULUM.md)

## What this module installs

A priced round is not a valuation — it is a preferred-stock contract, negotiated line by line against a reference document the industry has already agreed is "market." The National Venture Capital Association publishes that reference: seven signed model documents (term sheet, Stock Purchase Agreement, Investors' Rights Agreement, Right of First Refusal / Co-Sale Agreement, Voting Agreement, Model Certificate of Incorporation, Management Rights Letter) that thousands of Series-A financings are drafted against every year. The clauses in a term sheet are legible in that reference. What matters for the CFO is which of them are market-standard silence, which are moderately negotiated, which are aggressive, and which are unusual — and where the economic consequence of each clause lands on the founder, the common employees, and the preferred stack over the company's life.

This module installs the CFO-grade term-sheet-reading and term-sheet-negotiating skill on top of the fundraise execution covered in [mod-107](../mod-107-fundraising-strategy-and-investor-targeting/) and the priced-round pricing covered in [mod-106](../mod-106-startup-valuation-frameworks/). Specifically:

- **The NVCA reference stack** — the seven model documents, what each governs, how counsel uses them as the starting draft, and the reading discipline of "market-standard vs. aggressive vs. unusual" against the current-quarter deal-terms data.
- **Liquidation preferences** — 1x non-participating (the Series-A market-standard), 1x participating (the double-dip that has been receding from Series-A but still appears in later rounds and in stressed markets), capped participation (participation up to a cap, non-participating above), senior vs. pari-passu stacks across rounds, and the exit-price crossover points that determine which preference structure wins for the preferred and which wins for the common.
- **Preferred-stock waterfall math** — the actual arithmetic that converts exit price into per-line dollars for a stack of preferred plus common plus options plus warrants across multiple structures. Why the "sale price above the last round's valuation" can still shortchange the common shareholders under a participating stack. The mid-band exit prices where the founder walks with materially less than the headline "per-share price at Series-B implies I'm worth $X."
- **Anti-dilution provisions** — broad-based weighted-average (the market-standard, moderate down-round protection), narrow-based weighted-average (harsher), full ratchet (rare and punitive) — how each recalculates the preferred conversion price after a down round, and what "pay-to-play" combined with anti-dilution actually does to the cap table.
- **Pro-rata rights** — the basic pro-rata (right to invest in future rounds proportional to current ownership), super-pro-rata (the right to invest above pro-rata), lead-pro-rata (larger allocation reserved for the lead), and the compression math that shows how existing investor pro-rata absorbs new-investor allocation in the next round.
- **Protective provisions** — the enumerated list of decisions requiring preferred consent (sale of the company, next-round terms, budget, hiring officers, incurring debt above a threshold, etc.), and how a founder distinguishes a market-standard list from a veto-heavy list.
- **Board composition** — the seed board (founder-heavy, sometimes founder-only), the Series-A board (typically 2 founder + 1 Series-A + 2 independents in the current market), the Series-B board (dilution of founder seats, addition of independents and observer rights), and the mechanics of independent-director selection under the Voting Agreement.
- **Drag-along, tag-along, redemption, dividend accrual, and MFN** — the "quiet" clauses that get less attention in the CEO's line-by-line review but have real consequences at exit, in a down round, or in a bridge. Which of these are market-standard silence and which are actively negotiated.
- **The quarterly market-conditions read** — Fenwick & West Silicon Valley Venture Survey, Wilson Sonsini Entrepreneurs Report, Carta liquidation-preference data — used as the market-context reference behind every term-sheet call. In investor-favourable quarters, "market standard" drifts toward more investor protection; in founder-favourable quarters, the drift reverses. The CFO's job is to read the current-quarter position and to negotiate against it.

The module deliberately covers **term-sheet mechanics** on top of the fundraise-execution and valuation work in mod-106 and mod-107. Down-round mechanics, pay-to-play, and bridge-financing structure live in [mod-109](../mod-109-runway-management-and-bridge-financing/). Board governance downstream of the composition decision lives in [mod-110](../mod-110-board-and-investor-governance-for-the-cfo/).

It teaches the term-sheet clauses, their economic consequences, and the negotiation discipline that produces a defensible executed term sheet.
