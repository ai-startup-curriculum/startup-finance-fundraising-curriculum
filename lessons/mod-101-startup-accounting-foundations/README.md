# mod-101 — Startup Accounting Foundations

**Cash vs. Accrual, ASC 606, and the SaaS Bookings-Billings-Revenue Stack**

**Track:** [Startup Finance & Fundraising](../../CURRICULUM.md)
**Stage:** IDEA / PRE-SEED
**Hours:** 18 (7 chapters + 6 exercises + 1 lab + 1 quiz)

## What this module installs

The startup-accounting mental model a founder or CFO needs before *any* other financial decision — cohort economics (mod-102), driver-based forecasting (mod-103), cap tables (mod-104), fundraising (mod-107), board reporting (mod-110) — becomes coherent. Specifically:

- The three-statement financials (P&L, balance sheet, cash-flow statement) produced on both cash and accrual basis, and why they diverge for a SaaS startup with annual-prepay concentration.
- The ASC 606 five-step revenue-recognition model applied to SaaS subscriptions, implementation, training, and usage-based add-ons.
- The four SaaS numbers — bookings, billings, revenue, cash — that a CFO reports separately and a founder frequently conflates.
- The deferred-revenue liability that carries the gap between billings and revenue, and the roll-forward that reconciles it.
- The gross-vs.-net revenue and contra-revenue treatment under the ASC 606 agent-vs.-principal framework — the reason a $10M-of-cash SaaS vendor and a $10M-of-GMV marketplace can report very different revenue lines.
- The cash-to-accrual transition triggers (audit, tax threshold, board request, IPO prep) and the decision-memo pattern for the CFO recommending the move.
- The authoritative guidance stack (FASB ASC, IRC / IRS, AICPA FRF for SMEs) and where startup-specific practitioner content fits alongside the primary sources.

## How this module fits

Every downstream module in the track assumes the accrual-basis financial vocabulary this module installs. mod-102 (unit economics) treats revenue, gross margin, and CAC as accrual concepts. mod-103 (three-statement model) is fundamentally an accrual construction. mod-107 (fundraising) and mod-110 (board governance) present accrual-basis financials to investors and boards. mod-111 (finance operations) picks up from here to design the month-end close and the audit-readiness workstream this module's mechanics feed.

The module *does not* teach: cohort unit economics (that is mod-102), driver-based model construction (mod-103), or controller-level bookkeeping mechanics (a future `startup-accounting-controllership` track would own that depth). It teaches the CFO-level *what to do* and the primary-source authority behind each choice.
