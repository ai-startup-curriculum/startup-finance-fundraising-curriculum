# Prerequisites — Startup Finance & Fundraising track

**Role level:** 40 (deep functional pillar — Startup family)
**Track:** `startup-finance-fundraising-curriculum`

This track is the **deep functional pillar** for the capital branch of the startup dependency graph. It assumes the learner already has the Foundations mental model installed and is ready to go deep on Startup Accounting → Unit Economics → Financial Modeling → Cap Tables & Equity Comp → Convertibles → Valuation → Fundraising → Term Sheets → Runway & Bridge → Board Governance → Finance Operations. The eleven modules move a finance leader from a founder-bookkeeping cash-basis P&L to a pre-IPO CFO's board pack, term-sheet red-line, and finance-org staging plan.

## Assumed lower-level curriculum

- **[`startup-foundations`](https://github.com/ai-startup-curriculum/startup-foundations)** (level 10) — required. Foundations owns the *system* (what a startup is), the *graph* (customer-facing / corporate-structure / economics branches), the *stages* (IDEA → PRE-SEED → SEED → SERIES-A → GROWTH → MATURE), the founder's operating loop (decide → allocate → communicate → measure), and the founder-numbers slice (runway, burn, growth read, default alive / default dead, North-Star metric selection). This track *never* re-teaches those; every module references back and layers CFO-grade cohort-modelling / capital-structure depth on top. If you have not completed Foundations, start there — this track will feel over-scoped and under-anchored without it.

## Assumed working knowledge

Even after Foundations, you should be practically comfortable with:

- **Spreadsheet fluency for financial modelling** — mod-102 (unit economics), mod-103 (three-statement model), mod-104 (cap tables and waterfall), mod-108 (waterfall across preference stacks), and every project rely on a spreadsheet as the primary artifact. Google Sheets, Excel, or Numbers are all fine, but plan on writing formulas that reconcile monthly across three financial statements — not just filling in a template.
- **Basic accounting literacy** — a comfort with debits and credits, the double-entry insight that the balance sheet must balance, and the P&L → balance-sheet → cash-flow-statement links. mod-101 covers the startup-specific pieces (ASC 606, deferred revenue, bookings-vs.-billings-vs.-revenue-vs.-cash) but assumes you can read a T-account.
- **Comfort with legal document text** — mod-105 (convertibles), mod-108 (term sheets), and every project require reading real term sheets, SAFEs, notes, and the NVCA reference documents at the clause level. You do not need to be a lawyer — that is what corporate counsel is for — but you need to be able to read a clause and reason about its cap-table and waterfall implications. If you have never read a term sheet, plan to spend extra time on mod-108's NVCA-teardown drill.
- **Reading long-form practitioner writing critically** — Brad Feld / Jason Mendelson (Venture Deals), Scott Kupor (Secrets of Sand Hill Road), Fred Wilson (AVC), Bill Gurley (Above the Crowd), Mark Suster (Both Sides of the Table), Jason Lemkin (SaaStr), First Round Review. You should be able to pull the underlying argument out of a term-sheet essay or a CFO-partnership post without being carried by the prose.
- **Ability to write a one-page memo and a board-pack slide deck** — mod-107 (fundraising strategy) and mod-110 (board governance) both ship written and slide artifacts. If you have never authored a board pack or an investor update, plan to spend extra time on mod-110's lab.
- **Willingness to work against a real (or realistic hypothetical) startup with a specific ARR / burn / cap-table shape** — every module has exercises and every project has a deliverable that only becomes concrete when applied to one company. Pick one before starting mod-101 and use it end-to-end.
- **Comfort with venture-market vocabulary from Foundations** — round stages (pre-seed, seed, Series-A / B / C), the venture-fund partnership economics abstraction, LP / GP roles, the founder-alive-dead frame. Foundations installs this vocabulary; this track uses it as shorthand.

## Not assumed, taught here

You do **not** need prior experience with:

- Any specific revenue-recognition standard — ASC 606's five-step model, deferred-revenue treatment for SaaS, bookings-vs.-billings-vs.-revenue-vs.-cash reconciliation. mod-101 covers each at the operating level.
- Any specific unit-economics framework at CFO grade — cohort-based LTV with a discount rate, fully-loaded CAC per channel, cohort tables, gross-margin bridges, NRR / GRR walk, burn multiple / Rule of 40 / cash-conversion-score. mod-102 walks each end-to-end.
- Any specific financial-modelling architecture — driver-based three-statement models, top-down vs. bottom-up reconciliation, scenario / sensitivity analysis, KPI dashboard reconciled to the model. mod-103 covers each with runnable drills.
- Any specific cap-table mechanic — pre-money vs. post-money option-pool shuffle, ESOP top-up sizing, waterfall analysis under different preference stacks, 409A methodology, Rule 701 caps, equity-comp instrument choice (ISO / NSO / RSU, 83(b), early exercise, QSBS). mod-104 covers each with worked examples.
- Any specific convertible instrument — YC post-money SAFEs (four variants), pre-money legacy SAFEs, convertible notes, side letters, MFN clauses, stacked-convertible priced-round conversion mechanics. mod-105 covers each with a stacked-conversion drill.
- Any specific valuation method — Berkus, Payne Scorecard, risk-factor summation, VC method, revenue-multiple against public comps, growth-adjusted multiples, Rule of 40. mod-106 covers each with the quarterly deal-terms data as the market-conditions anchor.
- Any specific fundraising-motion architecture — round sizing, investor targeting, funnel management, pitch-deck architecture in the Sequoia / YC pattern, data-room construction, close choreography, CFO vs. CEO roles in the raise. mod-107 covers each.
- Any specific term-sheet clause — liquidation preferences (non-participating vs. participating vs. capped), anti-dilution (broad-based weighted-average, narrow-based, full ratchet), pro-rata / super-pro-rata, protective provisions, board composition, drag / tag / redemption / MFN. mod-108 covers each with NVCA-reference-based drills.
- Any specific bridge / alternative-capital instrument — bridge SAFE, bridge convertible note, pay-to-play, venture debt (SVB / TriplePoint / Runway / Hercules pattern), revenue-based financing (Pipe / Capchase / Founderpath / Arc), ATM offerings, Reg A+. mod-109 covers each with decision drills.
- Any specific board-governance artifact — monthly investor updates, quarterly board packs, board-meeting agenda design, decision memos, board consents, committee charters (audit, comp, transaction). mod-110 covers each with authoring drills.
- Any specific finance-ops stack — Mercury / Brex / Ramp on ops, QBO / Xero / NetSuite on ledger, Gusto / Rippling on payroll, Airbase / Ramp on AP, Coupa / Blackline / Workday Adaptive at growth. mod-111 covers stack selection by stage.

## Recommended reading before starting

- **Term-sheet + fundraising canon:** [Brad Feld & Jason Mendelson — *Venture Deals*](https://www.venturedeals.com/) (at minimum, the term-sheet and preferred-stock economics chapters before mod-108), [Scott Kupor — *Secrets of Sand Hill Road* (a16z companion)](https://a16z.com/secrets-of-sand-hill-road/) (the VC-partnership-economics and term-sheet chapters before mod-107).
- **YC canon:** [Y Combinator SAFE documents + primer](https://www.ycombinator.com/documents) (before mod-105), [YC Startup School — How to Raise Money lectures](https://www.startupschool.org/) (before mod-107), [YC Series-A Fundraising Guide](https://www.ycombinator.com/library/4A-a-guide-to-seed-fundraising) (before mod-107).
- **NVCA reference documents:** [NVCA Model Legal Documents](https://nvca.org/model-legal-documents/) — skim the term sheet, SPA, IRA, and Voting Agreement before mod-108 to have the reference vocabulary in mind.
- **Unit-economics + valuation canon:** [David Skok — SaaS Metrics 2.0](https://www.forentrepreneurs.com/saas-metrics-2/) (before mod-102), [a16z — 16 More Metrics](https://a16z.com/16-more-metrics-to-watch/) (before mod-102 and mod-103), [Meritech Public Comparables](https://www.meritechcapital.com/public-comparables) (skim the dashboard before mod-106).
- **Governance + operator-blog canon:** [Fred Wilson — AVC](https://avc.com/) and [Mark Suster — Both Sides of the Table](https://bothsidesofthetable.com/) — read a few essays on board dynamics, CFO-CEO partnership, and the fundraising cycle to have the operator vocabulary in mind before mod-107 and mod-110.
- **Cap-table + equity-comp canon:** [Carta Equity 101](https://carta.com/learn/equity/) and [Holloway Guide to Equity Compensation](https://www.holloway.com/g/equity-compensation) — skim before mod-104 for the pre-money-vs.-post-money mechanics and equity-instrument vocabulary.

## Not required, useful to have

- **Prior CFO / finance-leadership experience at a startup** — a first outsourced-CFO engagement, a founding-team finance seat, or an early-stage VP-Finance role. This track does not require it but the exercises are richer against a real context.
- **Prior priced-round or convertible-round experience** — either founder-side (raised a round) or investor-side (invested in one). mod-105, mod-107, and mod-108 assume zero exposure but you will move faster if you have some.
- **Prior board-pack authorship experience** — first quarterly board meeting, first investor update, first board-consent authoring. mod-110 assumes zero exposure but the pattern-recognition transfers.
- **Prior FP&A / financial-modelling seat** — investment banking, private equity, corporate FP&A, or a Big Four transaction-services rotation. This track's model-building is startup-specific (driver-based, cohort-driven, high-uncertainty scenario planning rather than mature-company variance analysis) but the muscle memory transfers.
- **Prior legal / paralegal experience with corporate-finance documents** — makes mod-104, mod-105, and mod-108 faster; the term-sheet-teardown drills lean on being comfortable in the document.
- **Familiarity with a modern cap-table tool** — Carta, Pulley, Shareworks, AngelList Stack. mod-104 uses spreadsheets as the primary artifact but a real cap-table tool's mental model makes the exercises faster.
- **Familiarity with a modern FP&A / financial-modelling tool** — Causal, Runway, Mosaic, Anaplan, Workday Adaptive. mod-103 uses a spreadsheet as the primary artifact but hands-on exposure to a driver-based FP&A tool lets you understand the graduation decision faster.

- **The startup-as-a-system mental model, the dependency graph, the stage axis, the founder's operating loop, and the founder-numbers slice** — owned by [`startup-foundations`](https://github.com/ai-startup-curriculum/startup-foundations) (level 10). Prerequisite; *never* re-taught here.
- **The GTM-operator view of CAC / LTV / payback / magic number as GTM instruments** — owned by [`startup-product-gtm-curriculum`](https://github.com/ai-startup-curriculum/startup-product-gtm-curriculum) (level 30). This track covers the cohort-based, discount-rate-adjusted, board-defensible modelling of the same numbers and the fundraising-narrative packaging; the GTM operator's decision framing lives there.
- **Equity-compensation *policy*** — option-grant guidelines, refresh grants, promotion grants, cliff / vesting norms, IC-plan structure, compensation-committee territory — owned by [`startup-operations-governance-curriculum`](https://github.com/ai-startup-curriculum/startup-operations-governance-curriculum) (level 25). This track owns equity *economics* — dilution math, option-pool math, waterfall analysis, 409A methodology, tax preference mechanics.
- **People / hiring / management / legal / general-corporate-governance depth beyond CFO-side board work** — owned by [`startup-operations-governance-curriculum`](https://github.com/ai-startup-curriculum/startup-operations-governance-curriculum) (level 25). This track owns the CFO-side of board and investor governance (updates, packs, decisions, committees); the CEO-side and the general-governance depth live there.
- **M&A / IPO / secondary-transaction / shutdown *transaction* mechanics** — owned by [`startup-exit-curriculum`](https://github.com/ai-startup-curriculum/startup-exit-curriculum) (level 40). This track owns the ongoing-company financial infrastructure and captable that feeds those transactions and the CFO-side dual-track audit-readiness pre-work; the transaction-execution mechanics defer there.
- **Controller / assistant-controller / staff-accountant / bookkeeping mechanical depth** — this pillar covers those at the CFO-decision level (which stack to buy, when to hire, when to audit) rather than the operator-mechanic level. A future `startup-accounting-controllership` track would own the operator-mechanic depth.
- **CEO-level fundraising-narrative, board-partnership, and capital-allocation framing** — owned by [`founder-ceo-curriculum`](https://github.com/ai-startup-curriculum/founder-ceo-curriculum) (level 30) as a sideways role-pathway peer that references this curriculum at ~70% coverage.
- **Legal opinion** — out of scope; route to counsel.
- **Personal tax / immigration / accounting** — out of scope; route to a qualified professional.
- **Investment advice** — out of scope; this track teaches the finance leader's framework, not personal or investor recommendations.
